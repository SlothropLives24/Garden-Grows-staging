Rules that apply to arugula

Do not plant brassicas in ground that held brassicas within 4 years (extension recommends 4-7, and even that is not a cure). Promising

Do not plant brassicas in ground that held brassicas within 4 years (extension recommends 4-7, and even that is not a cure).

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Plasmodiophora brassicae forms thick-walled resting spores that survive in soil more than 10 years. They germinate in wet, acidic soil (pH < 7); liming above ~7.2 suppresses infection. Spores outlast any garden rotation, so a host-free break reduces but does not clear inoculum.

Evidence: checked against 3 sources in the research-based literature.

The 3 sources

content.ces.ncsu.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Clubroot of Brassicas NC State Extension
Infested fields should be rotated away from brassica for a minimum of 4-7 years, but this is not guaranteed to prevent disease

vegetables.cornell.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Clubroot of Crucifers Cornell Vegetables
… rotation of at least 7 years out of susceptible crucifers may be effective.

www.umass.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Brassicas, Clubroot UMass Extension
Long (5-7 years) rotations between brassica crops
Remedy

Plant brassicas in different ground, or wait at least 4 years (extension recommends 4 to 7). Rotation alone is not a cure, because the resting spores outlast it. Raising soil pH above ~7.2 suppresses infection.

On ground that has carried brassicas within the rotation interval, a measured soil pH below about 7.2 leaves clubroot's door open; raising it is the lever every clubroot guide we read names, and the direction is all we give.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Plasmodiophora brassicae's resting spores germinate in wet soil below about pH 7.0, and alkaline soil with high calcium suppresses the infection; liming to 7.1 to 7.5 slowly reduces severity but is not reliable once an infestation is established.

Evidence: checked against 4 sources in the research-based literature.

The 4 sources

vegetables.cornell.edu Read by a person on September 2, 2026

Clubroot of Crucifers Cornell Vegetables
It is essential that hydrated lime be added each year unless the soil pH exceeds 7.5
Remedy

Raise this ground's pH toward 7.2 with lime well ahead of planting, using calcitic lime unless your magnesium is low (then dolomitic), and watch for boron shortage on coarse soils once the pH is up. Your lab gives the amount of lime, from a lime requirement test of your soil, and we don't. Rotation stays the first lever; this is the second.

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it A good hunch

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Resource concentration. A specialist locates and holds its host more efficiently at higher host density and continuity, so an unbroken run of one family across adjacent beds is an easier target than the same family separated by a non-host (associational resistance).

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Organization of a plant-arthropod association in simple and diverse habitats: the fauna of collards (Brassica oleracea) Root 1973
… the resource concentration hypothesis, which states that herbivores are more likely to find and remain on hosts that are growing in dense or nearly pure stands; that the most specialized species frequently attain higher relative densities in simple environments.

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Flea beetle movement in a broccoli monoculture and diculture Elmstrom, Andow & Barclay 1988
… strong, direct support for the hypothesis that host plants are harder to find and easier to lose in vegetationally diverse habitats than in monocultures.
Remedy

Where one family fills adjacent beds in a single season, breaking the run with a non-host family between them lengthens the distance the specialist must travel to the next host. The app discloses this and never docks a score. A continuous single-family block can be the right call anyway (a wind-pollinated crop like corn sets better massed), so the app names the trade-off and leaves the choice to the gardener.

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing. Promising

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner warns you.

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

A cover buys cool-season crops a week or two at the ends of the season. A good hunch

A cover buys cool-season crops a week or two at the ends of the season.

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Soil warmed by the sun by day is the heat source at night; a covering low to the ground traps that heat around the plants on calm, clear nights, keeping them a few degrees above the open air through the light frosts at each end of the season.

Evidence: checked against 6 sources in the research-based literature.

The 6 sources

yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

All About Cold Frames Iowa State University Extension and Outreach, Aaron Steil
The ground and frame of the cold frame are heated by the sun during the day and radiate that heat during the night, keeping the inside warmer. They are used to extend the growing season by two to four weeks in both the fall and the spring, allowing you to start plants outdoors earlier and continue growing them later in the season.

extension.colostate.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Frost Protection and Extending the Growing Season CSU Extension GardenNotes #722, Whiting, O'Meara and Wilson
In trials in Fort Collins, Colorado, a plastic cover on a frame typically provides 3°F to over 6°F of frost protection. It works well for cool season crops that are somewhat tolerant of frosty nights and adds two to six weeks or more on both ends of the growing season.

class.ucanr.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Frost Protection and Extending the Growing Season CSU Extension GardenNotes #722, Whiting, O'Meara and Wilson
Soil warmed by the sun in the daytime, is the source of heat for frost protection at night. Moist, smooth soil absorbs more heat. To trap heat from the soil around young vegetables at night, place a covering that is low to the ground and spreading. To recharge the heat source for the next night, any covering must allow sunlight to shine through to the soil or must be removed in the daytime.

Where frost is rare, sow so the crop grows through months that suit it. A good hunch

Where frost is rare, sow so the crop grows through months that suit it.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows and makes its harvest within a band of warmth: in months colder than its band it grows too slowly to finish, and in months hotter than it a cool-season crop bolts and turns bitter while a warm-season crop drops its flowers.

Hardy crops can go in later, counted back from the freeze they can't stand Promising

Hardy crops can go in later, counted back from the freeze they can't stand

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: A hardy or half-hardy crop's tissue survives the ice of a light freeze down to about 28 °F (-2 °C), so its season ends at the first night that cold, which comes after the first 32 °F frost, and later still where winters are mild.

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest. A good hunch

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows at the pace its days to maturity assume only in months at least as warm as the low end of its band, and in cooler months it grows too slowly to finish, so a frost-free season of cool months can end before the crop is ready.
