Rules that apply to corn

Corn needs a block of at least three short rows side by side for good kernel set, and four rows is better. Well established

Corn needs a block of at least three short rows side by side for good kernel set, and four rows is better.

Well established

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner won’t allow it.

How it works: Maize is wind-pollinated and protandrous (each plant sheds its pollen before its own silks are ready). Tassels shed pollen that must land on the silks of neighboring plants. In a single row much of the pollen blows out of the row, and each silk it misses is one missing kernel.

Evidence: checked against 5 sources in the research-based literature.

The 5 sources

extension.umn.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Growing sweet corn in home gardens UMN Extension
Always plant corn in blocks of at least four rows. Corn planted in a single row will have much of its pollen blown out of the row, and will produce ears that have blank areas where kernels did not form.

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Organic Sweet Corn Production NC State Extension
Corn is wind-pollinated and should be planted in blocks of at least 4 rows for good pollination to occur.

extension.umd.edu Read by a person on October 10, 2026

Growing Sweet Corn in a Home Garden UMD Extension
Plant in blocks of at least three to four short rows, rather than one or two long rows, to ensure good pollination and full kernel development.

hgic.clemson.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Sweet Corn Clemson HGIC 1308
This crop is wind-pollinated; therefore, plant in blocks of several rows rather than 1 or 2 long rows to ensure full ears.

richmond.ces.ncsu.edu Read by a person on October 3, 2026

Glass Gem Corn NC State Extension, Richmond County Center
When tassels start dropping pollen, stalks can be gently shaken. This mimics wind and will release the pollen to float down to the silks.
Remedy

Plant corn in a block of several short rows rather than one long row; four rows is best. For a small patch, shake the stalks gently while the tassels shed pollen, or dust a cut tassel over the silks, every few days. Or leave corn out and give the beans a trellis.

Two kinds of corn tasseling together spoil each other's kernels. Sweet corn pollinated by popcorn, field or ornamental corn goes starchy, and supersweet pollinated by any other corn loses its sugar. Keep different corn types 250 feet apart or 14 days apart in tasseling. Promising

Two kinds of corn tasseling together spoil each other's kernels. Sweet corn pollinated by popcorn, field or ornamental corn goes starchy, and supersweet pollinated by any other corn loses its sugar. Keep different corn types 250 feet apart or 14 days apart in tasseling.

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Corn is wind-pollinated and the pollen parent sets the kernel's endosperm (xenia), so a sweet-corn ear fertilized by a starchy type stores starch, and a supersweet ear fertilized by any other type reverts to the toughness of field corn. Standard, sugary-enhanced and synergistic sweet corns need no isolation from each other.

Evidence: checked against 7 sources in the research-based literature.

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself. Promising

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Cold hardiness. Hardy crops tolerate hard frost and half-hardy crops tolerate light frost, so they can occupy the ground weeks before frost-tender crops are allowed in. The offset is measured from the last spring frost.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing. Promising

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner warns you.

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

Where frost is rare, sow so the crop grows through months that suit it. A good hunch

Where frost is rare, sow so the crop grows through months that suit it.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows and makes its harvest within a band of warmth: in months colder than its band it grows too slowly to finish, and in months hotter than it a cool-season crop bolts and turns bitter while a warm-season crop drops its flowers.

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest. A good hunch

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows at the pace its days to maturity assume only in months at least as warm as the low end of its band, and in cooler months it grows too slowly to finish, so a frost-free season of cool months can end before the crop is ready.
