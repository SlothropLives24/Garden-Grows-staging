Rules that apply to sunflower

Sunflower residue suppresses germination of some small-seeded species. Promising

Sunflower residue suppresses germination of some small-seeded species.

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Sunflower tissue and roots release allelochemicals that persist in the soil and inhibit the germination and early growth of sensitive plants. Small-seeded, direct-sown neighbors are the ones at risk. A small seed has little reserve to push through the inhibition, whereas a transplant is already past the germination window and largely escapes.

Evidence: checked against 1 source in the research-based literature.

The source

pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov Read by a person on July 13, 2026

The Allelopathic Activity of Aqueous Extracts of Helianthus annuus L. ... on Germination, Development, and Physiological Indices of Pisum sativum L.
If a sensitive test plant is exposed to allelopathic compounds, its seed germination and seedling growth are reduced
Remedy

Keep small-seeded direct sowings out of last year's sunflower ground, or plant transplants there.

Do not plant lettuce or its family in ground that held it within 3 years, and know that lettuce drop's fungi live on most other vegetables too. A good hunch

Do not plant lettuce or its family in ground that held it within 3 years, and know that lettuce drop's fungi live on most other vegetables too.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Bottom rot (Rhizoctonia solani) and lettuce drop (Sclerotinia sclerotiorum and S. minor) survive in soil as mycelium and sclerotia for years and build where lettuce repeats; a three-year break holds bottom rot down. Drop's fungi infect alliums, brassicas, nightshades, cucurbits and legumes as well, so a lettuce rotation slows them without starving them, and only cereals are safe ground.

Evidence: checked against 4 sources in the research-based literature.

The 4 sources

extension.usu.edu Read by a person on September 2, 2026

Remedy

Plant lettuce and its family in different ground, or wait until 3 years have passed since this ground last held them. Where drop has shown, the break that counts is one of grains or corn, since most vegetables carry it, and dead plants go in the bin. Keep them out of the compost, because the sclerotia survive it.

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself. Promising

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Cold hardiness. Hardy crops tolerate hard frost and half-hardy crops tolerate light frost, so they can occupy the ground weeks before frost-tender crops are allowed in. The offset is measured from the last spring frost.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing. Promising

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner warns you.

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

Remedy

The evening before, cover the crops at risk with a sheet or frost cloth, never plastic, reaching the ground and loose at the bottom so the soil's warmth can rise under it, and take it off in the morning. A cover helps least in a long, hard freeze.
