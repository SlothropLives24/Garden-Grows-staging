Rules that apply to pear

Hardiness zone is the correct variable for perennials, and only for perennials. Well established

Hardiness zone is the correct variable for perennials, and only for perennials.

Well established

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Zones encode average annual minimum winter temperature, which governs whether a woody perennial survives to spring. It is the wrong quantity for an annual, which never sees winter. A zone that is too warm is a second, different mechanism. Mild winters do not kill a perennial; they fail to chill it. Deciduous fruit crops need a winter dormancy period to break bud and set fruit, so in a zone milder than a plant is adapted to it may survive and never crop. Only the cold end is enforced, because only winter survival follows from what a zone measures; the warm end gets advice and is never refused. That warm limit is a property of the cultivar, and that limits what this rule can do. Chill requirement is published per variety and varies enormously within a species, so a species-level ceiling says "the modeled range does not reach your zone", never "no plant of this kind will crop for you". So above its range the planner still offers the plant, with advice to choose a cultivar rated for your zone.

A self-incompatible pome fruit's pollen partner has to bloom at the same time and make viable pollen. A triploid apple cannot be the pollen source, and a second tree of the same variety is not a second cultivar. Well established

A self-incompatible pome fruit's pollen partner has to bloom at the same time and make viable pollen. A triploid apple cannot be the pollen source, and a second tree of the same variety is not a second cultivar.

Well established

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Cross-pollination only works while both cultivars' flowers are open together, so their bloom periods must overlap: early with mid, or mid with late. Early and late can miss each other entirely. A normal cultivar makes viable pollen; a triploid apple, with three sets of chromosomes instead of two, makes little or none and cannot fertilize a partner even though it needs one itself. Two plants of a single variety, including a colored sport of it, are genetically identical, so they reject each other's pollen exactly as a lone tree does.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.usu.edu Read by a person on September 1, 2026

USU Extension, Production Horticulture, Small Acreage / Fruit FAQ
If one cultivar is to provide pollen for another, their period of bloom should overlap, particularly early bloom.

yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu Read by a person on September 1, 2026

Pollination Requirements for Tree and Small Fruits Iowa State Extension
Most European pears are self-unfruitful. Plant at least two different cultivars.
Remedy

Pick the second cultivar so its bloom overlaps the first, and do not rely on a triploid apple (Gravenstein, Winesap, Mutsu or Crispin, Jonagold) for pollen. Grow one only beside two other cultivars that pollinate each other. An ornamental crabapple that blooms at the same time will pollinate any apple. Two strains of one variety do not count as two cultivars. The partner does not have to share the bed. Within 50 to 100 feet is close enough for the bees, so a neighbor's tree of a different variety counts. For apple, a crabapple in bloom at the same time does too.

Apple, pear and quince share fire blight, and the tree you plant decides most of the fight. Choose a resistant cultivar, hold back on nitrogen, and prune strikes out well below the damage.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Erwinia amylovora infects some 75 rose family species, pear worst, and moves between trees on rain splash, on bees and other insects drawn to its ooze, and on pruning tools; soft, fast growth from heavy pruning or excess nitrogen is what it infects most easily.

Fire blight UMN Extension

Remedy

Plant a resistant cultivar where you can: apples such as Liberty, Enterprise and Freedom; pears such as Moonglow, Magness and Kieffer. Avoid excess nitrogen. Cut strikes out at least 8 to 12 inches below the damage, in dormancy where possible, and clean the tool between cuts.

Tree fruits differ in whether a lone tree can set a crop. Apples and most European pears are self-unfruitful. One cultivar alone will not set a normal crop, so plant at least two different cultivars whose bloom periods overlap. For apple, a flowering crabapple in bloom at the same time also works. One cultivar alone will not set a normal crop, so plant at least two different cultivars whose bloom periods overlap. For apple, a flowering crabapple in bloom at the same time also works. European plums are partially to entirely self-fruitful, and a second European plum cultivar makes fruit set reliable. Japanese and hybrid plums will not cross-pollinate them. Peaches and sour cherries are self-fruitful and may be planted alone. Partner trees must stand near each other. The published distances are within 50 to 100 feet, and 300 feet at most.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A self-unfruitful tree will not set a normal crop from its own pollen or the pollen of another tree of the same cultivar; it needs pollen from a genetically different, compatible cultivar, carried by bees while both trees are in bloom. That is why the partner must be a different cultivar, must bloom at the same time, and must stand within a bee's working range. It is also why a self-fruitful species like peach or sour cherry, whose own pollen sets fruit, bears alone.

Fruitfulness and Pollination Univ. of Illinois Extension

yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

Pollination Requirements for Tree and Small Fruits Iowa State Univ. Extension
Fruit trees that require two different varieties for pollination should be planted within 50 to 100 feet of one another to ensure a good fruit set

extension.psu.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

Pollination Requirements for Various Fruits and Nuts Penn State Extension
Apple: Cross-pollination is always needed to produce adequate fruit crop
Remedy

Check the pollination requirement before buying. For apple and pear, plant two different cultivars with overlapping bloom. Early- and late-blooming cultivars may miss each other, and for apple a flowering crabapple that blooms at the same time also serves. For European plum, pair with a second European plum cultivar. A Japanese or hybrid plum won't do. Site partner trees close together, within 50 to 100 feet where you can and within 300 feet at most. A peach or sour cherry needs no partner.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing. Promising

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner warns you.

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

Remedy

The evening before, cover the crops at risk with a sheet or frost cloth, never plastic, reaching the ground and loose at the bottom so the soil's warmth can rise under it, and take it off in the morning. A cover helps least in a long, hard freeze.

A cover buys cool-season crops a week or two at the ends of the season. A good hunch

A cover buys cool-season crops a week or two at the ends of the season.

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Soil warmed by the sun by day is the heat source at night; a covering low to the ground traps that heat around the plants on calm, clear nights, keeping them a few degrees above the open air through the light frosts at each end of the season.

Evidence: checked against 6 sources in the research-based literature.

The 6 sources

yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

All About Cold Frames Iowa State University Extension and Outreach, Aaron Steil
The ground and frame of the cold frame are heated by the sun during the day and radiate that heat during the night, keeping the inside warmer. They are used to extend the growing season by two to four weeks in both the fall and the spring, allowing you to start plants outdoors earlier and continue growing them later in the season.

extension.colostate.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Frost Protection and Extending the Growing Season CSU Extension GardenNotes #722, Whiting, O'Meara and Wilson
In trials in Fort Collins, Colorado, a plastic cover on a frame typically provides 3°F to over 6°F of frost protection. It works well for cool season crops that are somewhat tolerant of frosty nights and adds two to six weeks or more on both ends of the growing season.

class.ucanr.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Frost Protection and Extending the Growing Season CSU Extension GardenNotes #722, Whiting, O'Meara and Wilson
Soil warmed by the sun in the daytime, is the source of heat for frost protection at night. Moist, smooth soil absorbs more heat. To trap heat from the soil around young vegetables at night, place a covering that is low to the ground and spreading. To recharge the heat source for the next night, any covering must allow sunlight to shine through to the soil or must be removed in the daytime.

extension.usu.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Extending the Garden Season Utah State University Extension, Beddes, Caron, Hansen and Gunnell
Low = Plant 1–2 weeks before suggested planting date; Medium = 2–4 weeks before suggested planting date; High = 4–8 weeks before average planting date

hort.extension.wisc.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Floating Row Cover University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension, Wisconsin Horticulture, Susan Mahr
Depending on the weight of the covering you choose, you can gain between 2 and 8 degrees of frost protection or warm the local environment sufficiently to harvest certain crops a week or two early.

uaex.uada.edu Read by a person on October 14, 2026

Row covers for vegetable gardening University of Arkansas System Division of Agriculture, Cooperative Extension Service
Clear plastic is useless in providing frost protection because the radiant energy released from the soil on those frosty nights passes right through the covering and never warms the air around the plants.
Remedy

Sow or set out cool-season crops under the cover that many weeks ahead of the open-ground date, and keep autumn crops covered past the usual last call. Vent on sunny days, keep plastic off the leaves, and close the cover before sunset.
