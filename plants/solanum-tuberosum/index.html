Rules that apply to potato

Don't plant nightshades where nightshades grew in the last 3 years Promising

Don't plant nightshades where nightshades grew in the last 3 years

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Alternaria solani (early blight) and Septoria lycopersici overwinter on tomato debris and solanaceous weeds; a 2-3 year break plus debris removal starves this inoculum. Fusarium has a very narrow host range, so rotating away from the host is effective. Verticillium is the exception. Its microsclerotia persist ~10 years and it infects 300+ host families, so a garden-scale rotation does not control it.

Evidence: checked against 5 sources in the research-based literature.

The 5 sources

hgic.clemson.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Tomato Diseases & Disorders Clemson HGIC
… tomatoes and related vegetables, such as potatoes, peppers, and eggplants, should not be planted on the same land more than once in three years.

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Early Blight of Tomato NC State Extension

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Septoria Leaf Spot of Tomato NC State Extension
The fungus is known to overwinter on infected tomato debris and solanaceous weed hosts

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Verticillium Wilt of Tomato and Eggplant NC State Extension
The pathogen can survive in the soil without a host for up to 10 years in the form of structures called microsclerotia

umass.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Remedy

Plant nightshades in different ground, or wait until 3 years have passed since this ground last held them. Clear and remove the old debris either way, because the inoculum overwinters on it.

Limed ground is the wrong ground for the next potato crop, because common scab worsens as soil pH rises and lime raises it. Promising

Limed ground is the wrong ground for the next potato crop, because common scab worsens as soil pH rises and lime raises it.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Common scab (Streptomyces scabies) infects young tubers through the lenticels and is favored as soil goes less acid; at about pH 5.2 and below it is largely suppressed. Lime raises pH and its effect lasts years, so ground limed for a crop that wants sweet soil carries a scab-prone pH into the seasons that follow.

Evidence: checked against 5 sources in the research-based literature.

The 5 sources

Remedy

This ground has been limed, and potatoes want it sour. Scab is skin-deep and the tubers still eat, but where it has been a problem, put the potatoes on ground you have not limed, choose a scab-resistant variety, keep the soil evenly moist for the four to nine weeks after planting while the tubers set, and never lime or add wood ash where potatoes are going next. A later pH reading at or below about 5.2 on this ground would quiet this. We will not give a sulfur rate to get there, because the amount is your lab's to give: ask it for its sulfur recommendation for your soil.

Late blight comes back from last year's potatoes. It does not come back from the soil. Tubers left in the ground carry it through winter as volunteers, so ground that grew potatoes last season needs its volunteers pulled before this year's tomatoes or potatoes go in.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Phytophthora infestans needs living tissue to overwinter. Infected tubers left in the ground, in a compost heap that does not freeze, or in a cull pile survive and sprout as volunteers that carry the blight into the new season; tomatoes do not carry it, because frost kills the whole plant and the seed is clean.

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it A good hunch

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Resource concentration. A specialist locates and holds its host more efficiently at higher host density and continuity, so an unbroken run of one family across adjacent beds is an easier target than the same family separated by a non-host (associational resistance).

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Organization of a plant-arthropod association in simple and diverse habitats: the fauna of collards (Brassica oleracea) Root 1973
… the resource concentration hypothesis, which states that herbivores are more likely to find and remain on hosts that are growing in dense or nearly pure stands; that the most specialized species frequently attain higher relative densities in simple environments.

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Flea beetle movement in a broccoli monoculture and diculture Elmstrom, Andow & Barclay 1988
… strong, direct support for the hypothesis that host plants are harder to find and easier to lose in vegetationally diverse habitats than in monocultures.
Remedy

Where one family fills adjacent beds in a single season, breaking the run with a non-host family between them lengthens the distance the specialist must travel to the next host. The app discloses this and never docks a score. A continuous single-family block can be the right call anyway (a wind-pollinated crop like corn sets better massed), so the app names the trade-off and leaves the choice to the gardener.

Potato tubers that catch light while growing (wherever soil settles or washes away and shallow tubers are left uncovered) turn green and become bitter and toxic, so the crop must be kept covered with soil. Promising

Potato tubers that catch light while growing (wherever soil settles or washes away and shallow tubers are left uncovered) turn green and become bitter and toxic, so the crop must be kept covered with soil.

Promising

This one is about eating safely, not how the plant grows. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Tuber tissue exposed to light produces chlorophyll and, in that same tissue, synthesises the toxic glycoalkaloids solanine and chaconine, so soil mounded over the developing tubers keeps light off them and prevents both the greening and the toxin from forming.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.psu.edu Accessed August 21, 2026

Potatoes in the Garden and the Kitchen Penn State Extension

extension.umn.edu Read by a person on October 2, 2026

Growing potatoes in home gardens UMN Extension
Green skin occurs when potato tubers experience exposure to light
Remedy

Hill the crop. Once the stems are roughly 8-12 inches (20-30 cm) tall, draw loose soil up around the base of the plants to cover most of the stem and bury any shallow tubers, and repeat two or three times through the season so the tubers stay covered. Green tubers are bitter and should not be eaten; the green signals the toxin is present and is not reliably removed by peeling, so the defense is to keep light off the tubers in the first place, in the ground and again in dark storage.

Potatoes are grown from certified seed potatoes (disease-free seed tubers from a certified source). Don't plant saved tubers or grocery-store potatoes. Large seed tubers are cut into blocky pieces, each with at least one eye, and the cut surfaces left to dry to a tough protective skin before planting. Grocery-store potatoes may be treated to stay dormant and can carry disease that persists in the soil.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Potato is propagated vegetatively (the crop grows from a piece of tuber), so any virus or disease in the seed tuber is passed straight to the new plants and can remain in the soil for years; certified seed is inspected, disease-free stock, which is why saving one's own tubers or planting grocery potatoes risks introducing disease. Each planted piece must carry an eye, the bud the new shoot grows from. A freshly cut piece exposes moist flesh that storage and soil rots colonise; holding the cut pieces a few days until the surface dries and forms a corky protective layer (suberises) seals that wound before planting. Grocery tubers may additionally be treated with a sprout inhibitor, so they are slow to grow.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.umn.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

Growing potatoes in home gardens UMN Extension
Buy disease-free seed tubers from a certified grower or seed distributor

extension.usu.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

How to Grow Potatoes in Your Garden Utah State Univ. Extension
When buying seed tubers, ask for certified seed as this will help reduce the potential for introducing disease into the garden
Remedy

Buy certified seed potatoes. Cut large ones into blocky pieces of about two ounces, each with at least one or two eyes; leave small tubers whole. Spread the cut pieces at room temperature (about 60-70 °F) for a few days until the cut surface has dried and formed a tough skin, then plant. Do not plant grocery-store potatoes (possible sprout inhibitor, and disease that lingers in the soil) and do not replant your own saved tubers year to year (virus and disease build up).

Brown leaf spots with target-like rings, starting on the lower leaves of tomato or potato, are early blight, a fungus that overwinters in plant debris and soil and reaches the leaves by soil splash.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: The early blight fungi overwinter in infected plant debris and in the soil. Lower leaves are infected when they touch contaminated soil or when rain splashes soil up onto them; the spores need free water or very high humidity to germinate. Spots enlarge with concentric rings, and fruit can form leathery black spots.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

A cover buys cool-season crops a week or two at the ends of the season.

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Soil warmed by the sun by day is the heat source at night; a covering low to the ground traps that heat around the plants on calm, clear nights, keeping them a few degrees above the open air through the light frosts at each end of the season.

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest. A good hunch

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows at the pace its days to maturity assume only in months at least as warm as the low end of its band, and in cooler months it grows too slowly to finish, so a frost-free season of cool months can end before the crop is ready.
