Rules that apply to eggplant

Don't plant nightshades where nightshades grew in the last 3 years Promising

Don't plant nightshades where nightshades grew in the last 3 years

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Alternaria solani (early blight) and Septoria lycopersici overwinter on tomato debris and solanaceous weeds; a 2-3 year break plus debris removal starves this inoculum. Fusarium has a very narrow host range, so rotating away from the host is effective. Verticillium is the exception. Its microsclerotia persist ~10 years and it infects 300+ host families, so a garden-scale rotation does not control it.

Evidence: checked against 5 sources in the research-based literature.

The 5 sources

hgic.clemson.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Tomato Diseases & Disorders Clemson HGIC
… tomatoes and related vegetables, such as potatoes, peppers, and eggplants, should not be planted on the same land more than once in three years.

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Early Blight of Tomato NC State Extension
Rotate every 2-3 years.

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Septoria Leaf Spot of Tomato NC State Extension
The fungus is known to overwinter on infected tomato debris and solanaceous weed hosts

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Verticillium Wilt of Tomato and Eggplant NC State Extension
The pathogen can survive in the soil without a host for up to 10 years in the form of structures called microsclerotia

umass.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Solanaceous, Verticillium Wilt UMass Extension
Verticillium wilt tends to affect a wide range of hosts and can infect many unrelated plants. Fusarium spp., on the other hand, have very narrow host ranges.
Remedy

Plant nightshades in different ground, or wait until 3 years have passed since this ground last held them. Clear and remove the old debris either way, because the inoculum overwinters on it.

Don't plant anything sensitive to walnut toxin (juglone) within a walnut tree's root zone. Nightshades are sensitive, and so are apple and cabbage. Well established

Don't plant anything sensitive to walnut toxin (juglone) within a walnut tree's root zone. Nightshades are sensitive, and so are apple and cabbage.

Well established

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner won’t allow it.

How it works: Juglans roots exude hydrojuglone, oxidized to juglone, a respiration inhibitor. The nightshades are highly sensitive; they wilt and die.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

Blossom-end rot comes from uneven watering, which stops calcium reaching the fruit. It is usually not caused by too little calcium in the soil. Well established

Blossom-end rot comes from uneven watering, which stops calcium reaching the fruit. It is usually not caused by too little calcium in the soil.

Well established

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Calcium reaches the fruit in the water the roots draw up, carried by the xylem along with that stream. When the supply of water is interrupted the supply of calcium is interrupted with it, and the enlarging fruit runs short at its tip, even where soil calcium is adequate.

Evidence: checked against 3 sources in the research-based literature.

The 3 sources

extension.umd.edu Read by a person on August 2, 2026

hgic.clemson.edu Read by a person on August 2, 2026

Remedy

Keep the soil evenly moist, without letting it dry out and then soaking it, and mulch to hold that moisture steady. We are not naming a calcium amendment or a rate, because the sources disagree on whether adding calcium helps at all, and any amendment number would need a soil test first.

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it A good hunch

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Resource concentration. A specialist locates and holds its host more efficiently at higher host density and continuity, so an unbroken run of one family across adjacent beds is an easier target than the same family separated by a non-host (associational resistance).

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Organization of a plant-arthropod association in simple and diverse habitats: the fauna of collards (Brassica oleracea) Root 1973
… the resource concentration hypothesis, which states that herbivores are more likely to find and remain on hosts that are growing in dense or nearly pure stands; that the most specialized species frequently attain higher relative densities in simple environments.

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Flea beetle movement in a broccoli monoculture and diculture Elmstrom, Andow & Barclay 1988
… strong, direct support for the hypothesis that host plants are harder to find and easier to lose in vegetationally diverse habitats than in monocultures.
Remedy

Where one family fills adjacent beds in a single season, breaking the run with a non-host family between them lengthens the distance the specialist must travel to the next host. The app discloses this and never docks a score. A continuous single-family block can be the right call anyway (a wind-pollinated crop like corn sets better massed), so the app names the trade-off and leaves the choice to the gardener.

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself. Promising

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Cold hardiness. Hardy crops tolerate hard frost and half-hardy crops tolerate light frost, so they can occupy the ground weeks before frost-tender crops are allowed in. The offset is measured from the last spring frost.

Long-season warm crops are started indoors several weeks before the last frost and set out after it. Tomato and tomatillo go about six weeks ahead, pepper and eggplant about eight, basil six. Promising

Long-season warm crops are started indoors several weeks before the last frost and set out after it. Tomato and tomatillo go about six weeks ahead, pepper and eggplant about eight, basil six.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A frost-tender crop with a long days-to-maturity cannot mature if it is only direct-sown after the last frost, because the frost-free window is too short. A head start under cover buys the weeks the season lacks; the seedling is set out only once frost has passed, so the transplant date is still frost-gated.

Very tender crops go out about two weeks after the average last spring frost: tomato, pepper, eggplant, lima beans, melons, watermelon, pumpkin and winter squash. Tender crops go in at the frost itself. Promising

Very tender crops go out about two weeks after the average last spring frost: tomato, pepper, eggplant, lima beans, melons, watermelon, pumpkin and winter squash. Tender crops go in at the frost itself.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Chilling. A very tender crop is hurt by cool weather as well as by frost: it needs daytime temperatures above 60 °F, and a week of days below 55 °F can stunt it. Setting it out about two weeks after the average last frost gives the days time to warm.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing. Promising

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner warns you.

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

Where frost is rare, sow so the crop grows through months that suit it. A good hunch

Where frost is rare, sow so the crop grows through months that suit it.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows and makes its harvest within a band of warmth: in months colder than its band it grows too slowly to finish, and in months hotter than it a cool-season crop bolts and turns bitter while a warm-season crop drops its flowers.

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest. A good hunch

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows at the pace its days to maturity assume only in months at least as warm as the low end of its band, and in cooler months it grows too slowly to finish, so a frost-free season of cool months can end before the crop is ready.

Evidence: not yet confirmed. It is cited to 8 sources, which a person has yet to read.
