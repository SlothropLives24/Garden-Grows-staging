Rules that apply to tomato

Don't plant nightshades where nightshades grew in the last 3 years Promising

Don't plant nightshades where nightshades grew in the last 3 years

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Alternaria solani (early blight) and Septoria lycopersici overwinter on tomato debris and solanaceous weeds; a 2-3 year break plus debris removal starves this inoculum. Fusarium has a very narrow host range, so rotating away from the host is effective. Verticillium is the exception. Its microsclerotia persist ~10 years and it infects 300+ host families, so a garden-scale rotation does not control it.

Evidence: checked against 5 sources in the research-based literature.

The 5 sources

content.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

umass.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Remedy

Plant nightshades in different ground, or wait until 3 years have passed since this ground last held them. Clear and remove the old debris either way, because the inoculum overwinters on it.

Tomato fruit set fails when night temperatures persistently exceed ~21 °C (70 °F); high day temperatures above 29-32 °C (85-90 °F) compound it. Well established

Tomato fruit set fails when night temperatures persistently exceed ~21 °C (70 °F); high day temperatures above 29-32 °C (85-90 °F) compound it.

Well established

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Sustained high day (>29-32 °C / 85-90 °F) and night (>21 °C / 70 °F) temperatures make pollen non-viable ("tacky") and impair its release; flowers go unfertilized within their ~50-hour window and abscise.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

ipm.missouri.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Understanding Tomato Fruit Set Univ. of Missouri IPM
When daytime temperatures exceed 85 degrees F or nighttime temperatures exceed 70 degrees F, pollination suffers due to pollen becoming "tacky" and non-viable.

extension.umd.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

Expect Poor Fruit Set in Tomatoes This Week UMD Extension (J. Brust)
Daytime highs of 90° F and above and nighttime lows only getting down to 70° F ... will cause blossom drop and fruit abortion in tomatoes.
Remedy

Wait it out. Fruit set resumes on its own once nights cool, and the plants make up the gap. Do not reach for shade cloth. It cools the day, and this failure is driven by the night temperature, which Missouri IPM says shading will not touch. If hot nights are normal where you garden, buy a heat-set cultivar next season.

Blossom-end rot comes from uneven watering, which stops calcium reaching the fruit. It is usually not caused by too little calcium in the soil. Well established

Blossom-end rot comes from uneven watering, which stops calcium reaching the fruit. It is usually not caused by too little calcium in the soil.

Well established

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Calcium reaches the fruit in the water the roots draw up, carried by the xylem along with that stream. When the supply of water is interrupted the supply of calcium is interrupted with it, and the enlarging fruit runs short at its tip, even where soil calcium is adequate.

A tomato transplant goes into the ground deeper than it grew in its pot, buried up to its first leaves. It does not go in at the soil line it came out of.

Well established

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A tomato's stem forms roots along whatever length of it is buried in moist soil. Setting the plant deep therefore converts stem into root, and the plant ends up with a larger root system than the one that came out of the pot.

Late blight comes back from last year's potatoes. It does not come back from the soil. Tubers left in the ground carry it through winter as volunteers, so ground that grew potatoes last season needs its volunteers pulled before this year's tomatoes or potatoes go in. Promising

Late blight comes back from last year's potatoes. It does not come back from the soil. Tubers left in the ground carry it through winter as volunteers, so ground that grew potatoes last season needs its volunteers pulled before this year's tomatoes or potatoes go in.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Phytophthora infestans needs living tissue to overwinter. Infected tubers left in the ground, in a compost heap that does not freeze, or in a cull pile survive and sprout as volunteers that carry the blight into the new season; tomatoes do not carry it, because frost kills the whole plant and the seed is clean.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

umass.edu Read by a person on September 2, 2026

extension.umn.edu Read by a person on September 2, 2026

Late blight of tomato and potato UMN Extension
P. infestans can overwinter in Minnesota if protected in potato cull piles
Remedy

From April to June, walk last year's potato ground and any compost or cull pile, pull every volunteer potato and put it in the bin. Don't compost it. Then plant.

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it A good hunch

Pests of one plant family spread more easily through a solid block of it

A good hunch

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Resource concentration. A specialist locates and holds its host more efficiently at higher host density and continuity, so an unbroken run of one family across adjacent beds is an easier target than the same family separated by a non-host (associational resistance).

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Organization of a plant-arthropod association in simple and diverse habitats: the fauna of collards (Brassica oleracea) Root 1973
… the resource concentration hypothesis, which states that herbivores are more likely to find and remain on hosts that are growing in dense or nearly pure stands; that the most specialized species frequently attain higher relative densities in simple environments.

Read by a person on August 21, 2026

Flea beetle movement in a broccoli monoculture and diculture Elmstrom, Andow & Barclay 1988
… strong, direct support for the hypothesis that host plants are harder to find and easier to lose in vegetationally diverse habitats than in monocultures.
Remedy

Where one family fills adjacent beds in a single season, breaking the run with a non-host family between them lengthens the distance the specialist must travel to the next host. The app discloses this and never docks a score. A continuous single-family block can be the right call anyway (a wind-pollinated crop like corn sets better massed), so the app names the trade-off and leaves the choice to the gardener.

Over-feeding a tomato with nitrogen grows a tall, dark-green, leafy plant that flowers late and sets little fruit. The plant pours its resources into vines and foliage instead of blossoms, so flowering is delayed or sparse and yield drops. It's an imbalance between vegetative growth (leaves and stems) and reproductive growth (flowers and fruit), and it comes from how you fertilize. It is a different problem from the heat that makes already-open flowers fail to hold, so keep the two apart. Promising

Over-feeding a tomato with nitrogen grows a tall, dark-green, leafy plant that flowers late and sets little fruit. The plant pours its resources into vines and foliage instead of blossoms, so flowering is delayed or sparse and yield drops. It's an imbalance between vegetative growth (leaves and stems) and reproductive growth (flowers and fruit), and it comes from how you fertilize. It is a different problem from the heat that makes already-open flowers fail to hold, so keep the two apart.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: High available nitrogen biases the plant toward vegetative growth. Abundant nitrogen keeps it building leaves and stems, and that leaf-and-stem growth takes priority over the switch into flowering, so flower initiation is delayed and fewer flowers form. The visible result is lush dark foliage with reduced and later fruit set, with the growth spent on leaves and stems.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

fieldreport.caes.uga.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

Remedy

Go easy on nitrogen, especially before fruit set; do not feed a tomato a nitrogen-heavy fertilizer at planting and hold off feeding again until flowers and small fruit have appeared. Let a soil test say what the bed actually needs. The planner doesn't guess at NPK numbers.

Brown leaf spots with target-like rings, starting on the lower leaves of tomato or potato, are early blight, a fungus that overwinters in plant debris and soil and reaches the leaves by soil splash. Promising

Brown leaf spots with target-like rings, starting on the lower leaves of tomato or potato, are early blight, a fungus that overwinters in plant debris and soil and reaches the leaves by soil splash.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: The early blight fungi overwinter in infected plant debris and in the soil. Lower leaves are infected when they touch contaminated soil or when rain splashes soil up onto them; the spores need free water or very high humidity to germinate. Spots enlarge with concentric rings, and fruit can form leathery black spots.

Early blight in tomato and potato UMN Extension
Larger spots have target-like concentric rings.
Remedy

Mulch the soil under the plants and stake them, to keep soil and leaves apart; water at the base and keep it off the leaves. Remove spotted leaves early, but no more than a third of the plant. Grow resistant varieties (often marked EB), rotate out of tomatoes and their relatives for at least two years, and remove or bury infected plants in the fall.

Lower tomato leaves that yellow after dark spots appear have a fungal leaf disease (early blight or Septoria leaf spot); leaves that yellow without spots, especially in containers, can be short of nitrogen. Promising

Lower tomato leaves that yellow after dark spots appear have a fungal leaf disease (early blight or Septoria leaf spot); leaves that yellow without spots, especially in containers, can be short of nitrogen.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Early blight and Septoria leaf spot are fungal diseases that begin on the lower foliage in wet weather as deep brown or blackish spots, followed by yellowing or browning of the leaves. Early blight has target-like rings, and Septoria has whitish centers and dark borders. Separately, tomato leaves can yellow from a lack of nitrogen, which is common in containers because frequent watering and low soil fertility leave little of it.

Tomatoes, peppers and eggplant set little fruit while the nights stay cool.

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Low temperatures cut the pollen's viability and slow the growth of the pollen tube, so the flowers drop without setting fruit.

Evidence: checked against 4 sources in the research-based literature.

Where frost sets the season, a crop still needs enough warm months to reach harvest.

A good hunch

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner suggests it.

How it works: A crop grows at the pace its days to maturity assume only in months at least as warm as the low end of its band, and in cooler months it grows too slowly to finish, so a frost-free season of cool months can end before the crop is ready.
