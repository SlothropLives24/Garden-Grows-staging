Don't plant a crop that can't mature before your frost-free season ends

How it works: The season's accumulated growing degree days (a running total of its warmth) against the total the crop needs to mature, or the crop's days to maturity against the number of frost-free days.

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself. Promising

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: Cold hardiness. Hardy crops tolerate hard frost and half-hardy crops tolerate light frost, so they can occupy the ground weeks before frost-tender crops are allowed in. The offset is measured from the last spring frost.

Borage is an annual that self-seeds freely and returns on its own from dropped seed, so a single planting typically reappears as volunteer seedlings year after year. Expect volunteers in and around where it grew. If you would rather it did not come up, remove the spent flower and seed heads before the seed drops and pull unwanted seedlings while young; otherwise leave a few heads and let it reseed. Promising

Borage is an annual that self-seeds freely and returns on its own from dropped seed, so a single planting typically reappears as volunteer seedlings year after year. Expect volunteers in and around where it grew. If you would rather it did not come up, remove the spent flower and seed heads before the seed drops and pull unwanted seedlings while young; otherwise leave a few heads and let it reseed.

Promising

If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works: An annual that sets abundant seed which drops and germinates in place, so one planting reappears and spreads by self-sown volunteers each season without being replanted. The control follows from the same mechanism. Seed that is removed before it drops cannot volunteer.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.illinois.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

Plant of the Week: Borage Univ. of Illinois Extension
This annual self-seeds wherever it is planted, so in most years, an abundance of borage returns.

plants.ces.ncsu.edu Accessed August 22, 2026

Borago officinalis NC State Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox
… the plant is also self-seeding and will remain in your garden year after year.
Remedy

To keep borage going, leave a few seed heads to ripen and drop. To limit where it comes up, cut or deadhead the flower heads before the seed sets and drops, and pull volunteer seedlings while they are young and shallow-rooted.

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing. Promising

Frost can form on a night the forecast low is a few degrees above freezing.

Promising

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner warns you.

How it works: On a clear, still night leaves lose heat to the open sky, so a leaf can fall to freezing and ice can form in it while the thermometer, a couple of meters up, still reads a few degrees above.

Remedy

The evening before, cover the crops at risk with a sheet or frost cloth, never plastic, reaching the ground and loose at the bottom so the soil's warmth can rise under it, and take it off in the morning. A cover helps least in a long, hard freeze.
