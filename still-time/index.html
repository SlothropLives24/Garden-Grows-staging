Can I still sow this? Days left before your first freeze

In the US, type your ZIP code; anywhere else, your city’s name. The tool counts the days from today to your first fall freeze, using the odds from the nearest NOAA station, or, for a city abroad, from a weather station near it or a weather model. Then it sorts the crops by whether they can still finish if you sow now: the ones that beat even an early freeze, the gambles, and the ones that have run out of season. A hardy crop, one that stands a light frost, is counted to the first hard freeze instead, a night of about 28 °F (-2 °C). Where frost sets the season and the months here are too cool for a crop, or a late sowing would ripen into cooler months, it says so beside the crop. Where frost is rare, it lists the crops in season now.

Nothing is sent anywhere. The lookup runs on your device against a table of NOAA weather station records and a weather model’s, offline once it has loaded.

Outside the US? Type your city’s name: every city with a frost page works here, and the frost pages list them by country. A postcode from elsewhere can match a US ZIP code and bring up a US town’s dates.

Why the fast end of the range, and why the 1-in-10 date

Every crop matures across a range of days: a radish in 22 to 30, a butternut in 90 to 110. This tool counts the fast end plus a week of slack, because that’s the best case for a late sowing, with good soil, a warm autumn and no setbacks. It measures that against your first freeze, which is also a range of dates. Half of years freeze by the median date, and 1 year in 10 the freeze comes earlier. A crop that only clears the median loses to an early freeze in some years, so the safe deadline is the 1-in-10 date.

What this tool will not do

It won’t call a weather service, make up a maturity for a crop that has no recorded one, or promise a harvest. Days to maturity shift with soil, weather and variety, and a first-freeze date is odds. It’s a timing guide built from records that shows which crops are worth the gamble.

In the US, the frost dates here are NOAA’s recorded 1991 to 2020 normals, unshifted unless the answer says they were corrected for terrain. For a city abroad, they are a nearby weather station’s own where one has a long enough daily record in NOAA’s GHCN-Daily, and otherwise modeled from the ERA5 weather model’s daily lows.
