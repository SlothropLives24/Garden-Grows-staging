A winter cover crop is sown in autumn a set time before the first fall frost

A winter cover crop is sown in autumn a set time before the first fall frost (about six to eight weeks for crimson clover, 30 to 45 days for hairy vetch, four to six weeks for cereal rye), so it roots and grows before winter.

Promising A person has checked it against its sources. If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

The line has a run for each of its 6 sources. A solid run’s date is the day a person read the source (1); a dashed run’s is the day the page was fetched and quoted (5).

How to read this
  • Each run of the line is one group, as long as its count.
  • A dashed run is a source dated the day its page was fetched and quoted, marked “Accessed”. A solid run is dated the day a person read it.
  • It is one line, wound from the middle out, in the order of the list.

How it works

The plants need time to germinate and grow before the first hard frost and short days force them into dormancy; sown too late, a cover crop neither covers the soil through winter nor survives to grow again in spring. Sown too early, crimson clover can go to seed in the fall and not regrow until the soil warms. Cereal rye tolerates the latest sowing of the three.

What to do about it

Sow within the crop's window before your typical first fall freeze: crimson clover six to eight weeks before it, hairy vetch 30 to 45 days before, cereal rye four to six weeks before (rye can go in later and still overwinter, with less growth before winter).

How much it matters: No garden yield or biomass figure is stated or estimated. One field study (UVM, cereal rye) found that sowing after its optimal window cut ground cover by 57 percent and biomass by 44 percent; that figure is for rye in Vermont only and is not carried over to a garden. The rule advises and never blocks.

The evidence

Evidence: checked against 6 sources in the research-based literature.

The 6 sources

extension.umd.edu Read by a person on September 25, 2026

Cover Crops For Gardens University of Maryland Extension
Fall-sown cover crops are typically planted in a two-month window, starting in mid-August and ending no later than mid-October. The plants should have enough time to germinate and grow before the first hard frost and short days force them into dormancy.

sare.org Accessed September 25, 2026

Managing Cover Crops Profitably USDA SARE
Seed six to eight weeks before the average date of first frost

sare.org Accessed September 25, 2026

Managing Cover Crops Profitably USDA SARE
Plant vetch 30 to 45 days before killing frost for winter annual management

hgic.clemson.edu Accessed September 25, 2026

Cover Crops for the Fall Clemson Cooperative Extension HGIC
Legumes should be planted 6 to 8 weeks before a killing frost, and non-legumes should be planted 4 to 6 weeks before a killing frost.

Accessed September 25, 2026

Managing Cover Crops Profitably USDA SARE
In many regions, seed winter covers at least six weeks before a hard frost. Winter cereals, especially rye, are an exception and can be planted a little later. If ground cover and N recycling needs are minimal, rye can be planted as late as the frost period for successful overwintering.

www.uvm.edu Accessed September 25, 2026

Optimize Your Cereal Rye Cover Crop with the Right Sowing Dates and Seeding Rates University of Vermont Extension, NW Crops & Soils
… the optimal window for sowing cereal rye is during the four-week window around the historic first frost date for that location.
