A young apple or stone-fruit tree planted where an old one of its kind stood tends to sulk - stunted, uneven, sometimes dying back - because the ground an orchard tree leaves behind is not neutral for the next tree of the same kind.

A good hunch · suboptimal if ignored → advise

How it works

Apple leaves behind a soil complex of fungi and oomycetes (Rhizoctonia, Ilyonectria, Phytophthora, Pythium) together with the lesion nematode Pratylenchus penetrans; the stone fruits leave the genus-wide version, Prunus replant disease, and in peach the ring nematode with the bacterial- canker organism drives its severe named form, Peach Tree Short Life. Either way a young tree of the same kind meets that loaded soil with a root system too small to outgrow it.

What to do about it

On old orchard ground, don't drop a new tree of the same kind into the same spot. Have the soil tested for nematodes first; choose a replant-tolerant rootstock where one exists (a Geneva-series apple, a 'Guardian' peach); set the tree in the old grassed row-middles rather than the old tree row; or replace the soil in the planting hole. Waiting alone is not treated as a cure - many other crops host the same organisms - so a rotation is at best partial and never a guarantee.

The evidence

Evidence: confirmed against 5 sources in the research-based literature.

the 5 sources

WSU Tree Fruit, 'Apple Replant Disease' (Hewavitharana, DuPont, Mazzola; peer-reviewed), treefruit.wsu.edu/crop-protection/disease-management/apple-replant-disease (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'In Washington State, the disease is caused by multiple species within several genera of fungi: Rhizoctonia, Ilyonectria; oomycetes: Phytophthora, Pythium and in some sites lesion nematode Pratylenchus penetrans'; 'Many other crops can host the pathogens in this complex, thus crop rotation is not a guaranteed strategy to lower potential disease pressure. However, a five year plus rotation to a non-woody crop can help reduce pressure'; 'Choose tolerant rootstock The most important cultural control you can use is a disease tolerant rootstock'; 'Planting in old inter-row grass lanes has improved the tree growth'; 'For small-scale, soil in the planting hole can be replaced with good quality soil from a non-orchard site.'

Clemson HGIC, 'Peach Diseases', hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/peach-diseases (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'Do not replant old peach tree sites with new peach trees'; 'Peach Tree Short Life This is a disease caused by the ring nematode, bacterial canker organism (Pseudomonas syringae), fluctuating winter temperatures, pruning the wrong time of year, and poor horticultural practices'; 'Guardian' is more tolerant of the ring nematode (the rootstock).

NC State Extension, 'North Carolina Production Guide for Smaller Orchard Plantings', content.ces.ncsu.edu/north-carolina-production-guide-for-smaller-orchard-plantings (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'land that has grown peaches should not be planted back to peaches for at least four to five years to avoid potential nematode problems, replant issues, and Peach Tree Short Life (PTSL) disease'; 'Rootstocks are also chosen for insect and disease resistance, such as to fire blight, and tolerance to replant disease.'

Penn State Extension, 'Orchard Site Preparation: Bio-renovation', extension.psu.edu/orchard-site-preparation-bio-renovation (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'Take soil samples for pH fertility and nematode analysis'; 'two successive plantings of rapeseed reduce nematode populations equivalent to an application of Telone.'

Khan et al., 'Characterization of soils conducive and non-conducive to Prunus replant disease', PMC8664177, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8664177 (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'Successive orchard plantings of almond and other Prunus species exhibit reduced growth and yield in many California soils. This phenomenon, known as Prunus replant disease (PRD), can be prevented by preplant soil fumigation or anaerobic soil disinfestation, but its etiology is poorly understood'; 'successive orchard plantings of Prunus species are subject to complex replant problems that cause serious growth and yield suppression in certain soils.' This is the genus source that extends the rule from peach to apricot, sweet and sour cherry, and plum.
