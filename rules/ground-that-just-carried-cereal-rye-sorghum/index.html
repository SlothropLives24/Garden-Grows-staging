Ground that just carried cereal rye or a sorghum cover crop is a poor seedbed for small seeds for a few weeks. As the residue breaks down it releases chemicals that stop small seeds germinating, and the smaller the seed the worse it fares; transplants and big seeds largely shrug it off.

Promising · suboptimal if ignored → advise

How it works

Rye and sorghum roots and residue release allelochemicals - benzoxazinoids from rye, sorgoleone from sorghum - that persist in the surface soil for weeks after the cover is killed. A small seed sown into that layer has little reserve to push through the inhibition; a large seed or a transplant is already past the germination window.

What to do about it

Wait about two weeks after killing rye and about four after sorghum before sowing small seeds, water an inch to leach the surface, or set transplants instead of seed. Big seeds and transplants can go in sooner.

How much it matters: Georgia saw very poor carrot germination after a sorghum-family cover and asks for four weeks and an inch of water; Iowa asks for ten to fourteen days after rye before corn and says susceptibility runs with seed size. No garden figure is invented; the two lead times are the sources' own, and the rye figure rests on corn and transplant timings plus the seed-size principle, not on a rye-specific small-seed trial.

The evidence

Evidence: confirmed against 4 sources in the research-based literature.

the 4 sources

Iowa State Univ. Extension, Integrated Crop Management, 'Cereal Rye Cover Crops, Allelopathy and Corn' (April 2014), crops.extension.iastate.edu/cropnews/2014/04/cereal-rye-cover-crops-allelopathy-and-corn (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): 'Research has shown that susceptibility to allelochemicals is indirectly related to seed size - the smaller the seed the more susceptible the plant'; 'Terminating rye 10 to 14 days prior to planting corn greatly minimizes the chance of a negative impact.'

NC State Extension, 'Weed Management on Organic Farms', content.ces.ncsu.edu/weed-management-on-organic-farms (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): its allelopathic cover crop table lists cereal (winter) rye, sorghum and sorghum-sudangrass hybrids; 'Small-seeded and slow germinating crops are more likely to be adversely effected by allelopathic cover crops than are large-seeded, rapidly germinating crops (Weston, 1996)'; rye residues 'reduced the emergence of annual ragweed by 43 percent, green foxtail by 80 percent, redroot pigweed by 95 percent, and common purslane by 100 percent.'

Univ. of Georgia Cooperative Extension, Bulletin 1498, 'Organic Cool-Season Vegetable Crop Rotations for the Southeast', extension.uga.edu/publications/detail.html?number=B1498 (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): 'as the sorghum crop residue decomposes, it releases chemicals that inhibit the germination of small seeds'; 'A direct-seeded crop with small seeds is particularly vulnerable to allelopathic chemicals'; 'we saw very poor carrot germination after a summer cover crop in the sorghum family'; 'If cover crops in the sorghum family are used before direct-seeded crops, wait four weeks for the cover crop to decompose and irrigate at least one inch to leach any allelopathic chemicals out of the soil surface.'

Univ. of Illinois Extension, 'Cereal rye/winter rye: the perfect garden-reset cover crop' (Flowers, Fruits, and Frass, 2024-10-07), extension.illinois.edu/blogs/flowers-fruits-and-frass/2024-10-07-cereal-ryewinter-rye-perfect-garden-reset-cover-crop (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): 'It needs to be terminated in mid to late spring before it goes to seed, about 2 weeks before the desired transplant date.'

Where this rule stops

Fires from the season log, which knows what grew here last; the manual family checkboxes cannot tell rye from corn and never fire it. The small-seeded class is derived, not declared: sown at 1.3 cm or shallower and not started indoors. A cover crop not marked with a residue lead is silent, not cleared.
