Pepper fruit set suffers when night temperatures stay above ~21C (70F); the same hot spell's high days compound it.

Promising · costly if ignored → warn

How it works

Warm nights above ~21C (70F) weaken pepper growth and, together with hot days, leave flowers poorly pollinated so they drop before setting fruit. Pepper carries a second heat driver this night threshold does not see - daytime highs above ~34C (94F) also abort the flowers - so this rule catches only the night half of a two-sided problem.

What to do about it

Wait it out - pepper sets fruit again once the nights cool. Shade cloth cools the day but not the night this rule fires on, so it will not fix hot-night drop; keep the plants watered and unstressed so they carry the flush that follows.

The evidence

Evidence: confirmed against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

the 2 sources

UMN Extension, 'Growing peppers', extension.umn.edu/vegetables/growing-peppers (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'Dry soil, temperatures above 90F, or night temperatures below 60F or above 70F, can weaken plant growth'; 'When Minnesota has hot days and hot nights, pepper flowers can drop, leading to less fruit production.' 70 F is 21.1 C and this field is Celsius, so the stored 21 is that sentence rounded to the nearest degree, the same rounding tomato's night figure carries.

Univ. of Missouri Extension, 'Growing Sweet Peppers in Missouri', G6372, extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6372 (fetched in-container 2026-09-03; read by the maintainer 2026-09-03): 'Excessively high or low air temperatures (higher than 94 degrees F or lower than 50 degrees F) can reduce fruit set of peppers by causing flowers to drop from the plant.' Pepper's flower drop is driven by hot days as well as hot nights; a night threshold sees only the one half.
