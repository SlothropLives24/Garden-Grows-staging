Sudden wilt with sawdust at stem holes means a squash vine borer

As recorded: A squash or pumpkin runner that suddenly wilts, with sawdust-like frass pushed out of holes in the stem near the wilt, has a squash vine borer inside. The larva feeds in the vine and cuts off its water.

Promising A person has checked it against its sources. If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works

The adult is a day-flying moth that lays its eggs singly at the base of the plant, on leaf stalks and on vines. The larvae tunnel into the stem to feed, and their feeding destroys the vine's vascular tissue, stopping water and nutrient movement, and that is the sudden wilt. As they feed they push frass out of holes in the side of the stem.

What to do about it

At the first sign of frass, carefully slit the vine lengthwise near the damage and remove the larva. On vining types, bury a few nodes along each vine so it roots there and survives an attack at the base. Floating row cover keeps the moths from laying eggs, but only where squash did not grow the previous year.

How much it matters: No loss figure is invented. One larva in the base of a vine can kill the whole vine, which is why burying nodes (extra roots past the damage) is a remedy; the source gives no rate. The rule advises and never blocks.

The evidence

Evidence: checked against 1 source in the research-based literature.

How we grade evidence

Well established

A good hunch

Contested
