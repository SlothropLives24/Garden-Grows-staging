A warm-season bean germinates from warm soil, whatever the calendar says

A warm-season bean germinates from warm soil, whatever the calendar says. Sown into cold ground it rots or emerges weak, even after the frost has passed.

Promising A person has checked it against its sources. If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

The line has a run for each of its 2 sources. Each one’s date is the day the page was fetched and quoted.

How to read this
  • Each run of the line is one group, as long as its count.
  • A dashed run is a source dated the day its page was fetched and quoted, marked “Accessed”. A solid run is dated the day a person read it.
  • It is one line, wound from the middle out, in the order of the list.

How it works

Warm-season legumes take up water fast at sowing (imbibition), and in cold soil that first rush of cold water injures the seed tissue before the seedling can establish (imbibitional chilling), so the seed rots or comes up damaged. Soil below about 15 °C (60 °F) at sowing depth is where this bites, and the frost-free date can arrive weeks before the soil is that warm.

What to do about it

Wait for the soil to warm to about 15 °C (60 °F) at sowing depth, which a soil thermometer reads in a minute, or start the beans in warm cells and set them out. Peas are the opposite and are out of scope here. Half-hardy, they germinate in cold soil and are sown early on purpose.

How much it matters: No date or yield figure is given, because soil warms on its own schedule at each site. The rule advises checking the soil, whatever the calendar says; it never blocks, because a gardener with warm ground or warm cells is right to sow.

The evidence

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.usu.edu Accessed August 14, 2026

How to Grow Beans in Your Garden USU Extension
Beans are warm weather vegetables that require soil and air temperatures above 60°F for best germination and plant growth

extension.umn.edu Accessed August 14, 2026

Growing beans in home gardens UMN Extension
Bean seed planted in cold soil may rot rather than germinate, and plant growth will be very slow in cooler weather
