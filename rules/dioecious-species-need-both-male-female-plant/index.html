Dioecious species need both a male and a female plant to set fruit.

Well established · costly if ignored → warn

How it works

A dioecious species carries male and female flowers on separate plants: some plants make only pollen, others only fruit. A lone plant, or a planting that is all one sex, sets no fruit at all, because there is no pollen to reach the female flowers. Fruit needs at least one male plant near the fruiting females. This is a different mechanism from the single-locus self-incompatibility of apple and pear - there a plant has both sexes and rejects only its own pollen; here whole plants are one sex - so it is a separate rule, not a widening of that one.

What to do about it

Plant at least one male alongside the female fruiting plant(s) - a single plant, or several of the same sex, will never fruit. A few named cultivars are self-fertile and need no separate male; if you grow one of those, one plant is enough.

How much it matters: Categorical, not a dose: without a pollen source a dioecious female sets no fruit. The floor is two plants (one male, one female); the engine cannot see a plant's sex any more than it can see a cultivar's identity, so it states the requirement rather than claiming it is met.

The evidence

Evidence: confirmed against 1 source in the research-based literature.

the source

Where this rule stops

Fires when a dioecious species is planted with fewer than two plants, reading the new pollination.dioecious field. It is kept separate from the self-incompatibility rule that governs apple and pear on purpose: dioecy (separate-sexed plants) and single-locus self-incompatibility (a plant that rejects its own pollen) are different mechanisms, and one rule cannot state both in one sentence without blurring them. Like that rule it states the requirement rather than verifying it, because the corpus does not model a plant's sex - so it says "you need a male pollenizer" rather than claiming you have one. Self-fertile cultivars are a per-cultivar exception the species-level field cannot carry; the species note records them.
