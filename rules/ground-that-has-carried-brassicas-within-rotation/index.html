On ground that has carried brassicas within the rotation interval, a measured soil pH below about 7.2 leaves clubroot's door open; raising it is the lever extension names, and the direction is all we give.

Promising · suboptimal if ignored → advise

How it works

Plasmodiophora brassicae's resting spores germinate in wet soil below about pH 7.0, and alkaline soil with high calcium suppresses the infection; liming to 7.1 to 7.5 slowly reduces severity but is not reliable once an infestation is established.

What to do about it

Raise this ground's pH toward 7.2 with lime well ahead of planting - calcitic rather than dolomitic unless your magnesium is low - and watch for boron shortage on coarse soils once the pH is up. How much lime is your lab's number, not ours: a rate needs the buffer pH on a report. Rotation stays the first lever; this is the second.

The evidence

Evidence: confirmed against 4 sources in the research-based literature.

the 4 sources

NC State Extension, 'Clubroot of Brassicas', content.ces.ncsu.edu/clubroot-of-brassicas (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): 'The pathogen germinates in wet soils when the soil temperature is between 18 and 25 ºC (64 to 77 F), and the soil pH is acidic (pH less than 7.0)'; 'Alkaline soils and soils with high calcium content suppress the potential of the pathogen to initiate infection'; 'Liming to raise soil pH to 7.1-7.5 can slowly reduce disease severity, although this strategy is not completely reliable when there is a well-established infestation'; 'Calcitic lime is recommended over dolomitic lime unless the field has low levels of magnesium'; host list 'broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, Chinese cabbage, cauliflower, collard, kale, kohlrabi, mustard, oilseed rape (canola), radish, rutabaga, and turnip.'

UMass Extension, 'Brassicas, Clubroot', umass.edu/agriculture-food-environment/vegetable/fact-sheets/brassicas-clubroot (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): 'The disease is favored by soils with a pH less than 7.0'; 'Maintain a high pH by regular applications of lime'; 'Calcitic lime is preferred over dolomitic, except where magnesium levels are low'; 'High pH can lead to boron deficiency in coarse soils'; 'occurs on broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, turnip, rutabaga, and radish.'

UConn IPM, 'Clubroot of Crucifers', ipm.cahnr.uconn.edu/clubroot-of-crucifers (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): 'Maintain soil pH at 7.0-7.2 where clubroot is a concern. Lime well ahead of planting and incorporate thoroughly'; 'Monitor boron where pH is raised, especially on coarse soils'; 'It affects crops such as cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, kale, collards, turnips, rutabagas, radishes, mustard greens, and other brassicas.'

Cornell Vegetables, 'Clubroot of Crucifers', vegetables.cornell.edu/pest-management/disease-factsheets/clubroot-of-crucifers (fetched in-container 2026-09-02; read by the maintainer 2026-09-02): host range - most susceptible 'cabbage, Chinese cabbage, Brussels sprouts, some turnips, wormseed mustard, and some species of candytuft'; medium 'kohlrabi, kale, cauliflower, collards, broccoli, rutabaga, seakale, some turnips and radishes'; mildly 'rape (canola), black mustard, some turnip and radish varieties, and tumble mustard'; very resistant or occasionally immune 'wintercress; or yellow rocket, horseradish, shepherd's purse, wallflower, dames violet, peppergrass, garden cress, stock, and some radishes'. On pH: 'soil pH should be adjusted to pH 6.8 with ground limestone'; 'It is essential that hydrated lime be added each year unless the soil pH exceeds 7.5'; 'the use of nitrogen fertilizers will reduce soil pH (nullifying the beneficial effects of liming).'
