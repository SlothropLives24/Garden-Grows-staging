A bed whose shorter side runs well past ~1.2 m

A bed whose shorter side runs well past ~1.2 m (4 ft) has interior ground that can't be reached from its edges.

Promising A person has checked it against its sources. If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works

A comfortable arm's reach is ~0.6 m, so ground more than twice that across can only be tended by standing on the bed. Standing on it compacts the soil and breaks the structure the bed exists to protect.

What to do about it

Make a wide area into several beds with a path between them, so you never step into a bed. In the planner each bed stands alone and can't be split into parts, so add another bed instead.

The evidence

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

Read by a person on July 25, 2026

Iowa State Univ. Extension, Yard & Garden FAQ, raised bed size
Beds accessible on both sides can be 3 to 4 feet wide

Read by a person on July 25, 2026

Univ. of Minnesota Extension, Raised bed gardens
… the reach of your arm is generally a good metric ... If you can access both sides, the bed can be up to five feet wide.

How we grade evidence

Well established
The rule names its mechanism and is settled, by sources a person has read and checked or by plain arithmetic and geometry.
Promising
The mechanism is named and the literature backs it. A few questions are still open.
A good hunch
Plausible by its mechanism, but field studies are thin, so treat it as a lean.
Contested
The evidence points both ways, or no study has tested it, so the planner does not enforce it.
Refuted
The claim did not survive its sources. It is kept so the folklore can be answered.
