Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost

Cold-hardy annuals can be sown or transplanted before the last spring frost (hardy crops about three weeks before, half-hardy about two weeks before). Tender crops wait for the frost itself.

Promising A person has checked it against its sources. If you skip this, it still grows, just less well. The planner suggests it.

How it works

Cold hardiness. Hardy crops tolerate hard frost and half-hardy crops tolerate light frost, so they can occupy the ground weeks before frost-tender crops are allowed in. The offset is measured from the last spring frost.

The evidence

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.illinois.edu

Time Your Vegetable Plantings by Cold Hardiness University of Illinois Extension
… withstand freezing temperatures and hard frosts without injury

extension.colostate.edu Read by a person on September 30, 2026

Vegetable Planting Guide Colorado State Univ. Extension, GardenNotes #720
As early as 2-4 weeks before the date of the average last spring frost

Plus 2 working notes on how the sources were read. Every note is in the data download.

Where this rule stops

For spring sowing only. The three- and two-week leads sit inside Colorado State's planting table (hardy 2 to 4 weeks, half-hardy 0 to 2 weeks before the last frost). The two-week lead is that table's earliest edge, so in a cold spring wait a little longer.

How we grade evidence

Well established
The rule names its mechanism and is settled, by sources a person has read and checked or by plain arithmetic and geometry.
Promising
The mechanism is named and the literature backs it. A few questions are still open.
A good hunch
Plausible by its mechanism, but field studies are thin, so treat it as a lean.
Contested
The evidence points both ways, or no study has tested it, so the planner does not enforce it.
Refuted
The claim did not survive its sources. It is kept so the folklore can be answered.
