Some plants can't fruit from their own pollen

Some plants can't fruit from their own pollen, so they need a second plant of a different variety nearby.

As recorded: Self-incompatible species require 2+ genetically distinct plants.

Well established A person has checked it against its sources. If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

The line has a run for each of its 2 sources. A solid run’s date is the day a person read the source (1); a dashed run’s is the day the page was fetched and quoted (1).

How to read this
  • Each run of the line is one group, as long as its count.
  • A dashed run is a source dated the day its page was fetched and quoted, marked “Accessed”. A solid run is dated the day a person read it.
  • It is one line, wound from the middle out, in the order of the list.

How it works

Many fruit trees carry self-incompatibility alleles at a single locus. When a pollen grain shares an allele with the pistil it lands on, the pistil rejects it and no seed sets. A plant's own pollen always shares its alleles, so it cannot fertilize itself. Neither can a second tree of the identical variety, which is genetically the same. Fertilization needs pollen from a genetically distinct plant.

What to do about it

Plant at least two, and where the species needs a second cultivar make them different varieties. Two of the same will not pollinate each other.

The evidence

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.psu.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Pollination Requirements for Various Fruits and Nuts Penn State Extension
Chestnut, Chinese: All are self-sterile. Two or more varieties or seedling trees are necessary to ensure adequate cross-pollination.

pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov Accessed July 11, 2026

Pollen-Pistil Interaction in Response to Pollination Variants in Subtropical Japanese Plum (Prunus salicina Lindl.) Varieties
Japanese plum exhibits gametophytic self-incompatibility (GSI), a prezygotic reproductive barrier where genetically related pollen is rejected by the pistil ... governed by a polymorphic locus (S) ... Self-incompatibility occurs when the same S-allele is expressed in the haploid pollen grain and the diploid pistil.
