A fourth question: the pages where frost is rare

Ruled on 16 November (D-459): a frost page that says frost is rare draws its ring (276 pages); one that says frost comes in some or most winters draws none (27). These are the drawings you ruled on: each place shows the answer its page gives, then the ring beside it.

Singapore, Singapore

No frost on record: 244 pages. The record holds nothing to mark, and the page says frost is rare. Ruled: this plain ring.

The page says now: Frost is rare here. The Singapore Changi International weather station, 17 km (10 miles) from Singapore, recorded no frost in its 11 winters on record from 1991 to 2020, so the growing season runs all year.

Mexico City, Mexico

The page says frost comes in some or most winters: 27 pages. The record’s middle year has no frost, so its ring is green all the way round, while the page’s answer, from a weather station or from the record’s own count of winters, says frost comes. Ruled: no ring on these pages. Both sentences are kept here for the record.

The page says now: Frost comes in most winters here. The Puente La Llave weather station, 9 km (6 miles) from Mexico City, recorded a frost in 10 of its 10 winters on record from 1991 to 2020. A frost-tender crop waits until after February 19, the weather model’s late frost date, and frost can come later than that. Hollows and low ground near your garden can frost later in spring and earlier in fall than the station does.

A fifth question, ruled: whose dates a plant page shows

Ruled on 16 November 2026 (D-460): the first of the three below. A plant page draws no ring until a place is known, then the reader’s own place, drawn in the browser. A team page and a guide follow the same rule. It is built in milestone 3b. The question and its three answers are kept here for the record.

A plant page is built once for everyone, so it knows no place. The board draws tomato’s year “for ZIP 55401” with a Change link. The drawing is the one under “One plant” below, unchanged; the question was what a reader sees before the page knows where they are.

  1. No ring until a place is known (ruled). The page shows the plant’s facts and one line: “See tomato’s year where you live”. A reader who has already looked up a place on this device gets the ring for that place, drawn in the browser by the same module, under “Dates for your place” with a Change link. No date on the page is ever someone else’s.
  2. A sample place, named. Every reader sees the ring for one stated place, as the board does, until they change it. The page always has its picture, and most readers first see dates that are not theirs.
  3. No dates: the plant’s own shape. A ring with no frost and no dates, only the plant’s line as a length (its weeks indoors, its days to first picking). A drawing the boards do not show: it would be drawn here first.

How it moves

Reload the page. Each crop’s line unfurls and the day’s dot sweeps round to its place, once, as on the canvas. With reduced motion switched on in your phone’s settings, nothing moves. Words never move.

The new moves, approved

Approved on 17 November 2026 (D-462), from this page, and built on every page with milestone 3b: a year’s ring drifts and its lines swell (the figure’s own sheet), a row’s arrow slides, a button lifts and a fold’s caret turns over (the site’s sheet). The motion sheet (“How it moves, on every page”) names the five; they are kept below as they were shown, each the canvas’s own rule with its own timing. With reduced motion switched on, none of them runs.

1. Drift: the ring moves a little slower than the page

Scroll this ring slowly up the screen and back. It travels 32 px less than the words around it, so it sits behind them. It follows the scroll itself, so it has no duration. In a browser with no scroll timelines (older Safari and Firefox) it stands still.

3. A ring’s line swells

Point at the green stroke, or at a crop’s line where it is filled in. It grows 3% from the ring’s centre in 280 ms and settles back. Nothing opens and nothing is told only on hover.

4. A button lifts

Point at a button. It rises 2 px in 160 ms; pressed, it dips to 97% as it does on the site now.

5. A fold’s caret turns over

Open and shut this fold. Its caret turns half a circle in 160 ms. The fold’s words do not move or fade, and a fold never opens by itself.

How to read this
  • The green stroke is the season with no frost.
  • The marked line is when frost is likely: more marks, more likely.
  • A line inside is a crop: it starts the day it is sown and its dot is the first picking.
  • It reads clockwise from January at the foot, with midsummer at the top.

Not drawn: the boards’ own markup also turns a ring’s frost marks by ten degrees as the page scrolls. The motion sheet does not list that move, so it is left out until you ask for it.
