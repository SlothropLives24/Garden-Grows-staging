November 2026

  • The Plan’s plant list gets shorter

    November 10, 2026 · The whole app

    Beside a plant team’s drawing, each kind of plant is now one line: its colored dot, its name and how many to plant.

    Where they go and how far apart are said once, above the lines that share them. “Design it yourself” keeps its full sentences.

    Try it ›

  • The app says less at once, and keeps the rest a tap away

    November 9, 2026 · The whole app

    Evidence opens on the folklore, or on your own garden’s rules, with the tabs and search behind “Browse everything”.

    Account’s settings lose their boxes. On the Plan, a team’s card shows its size and kind once you open it, and its note on how the plants get along folds behind its first line once something is planted. Dates keeps your own crops and the quick picks as chips and folds the rest by kind, and a plant’s page leads with the rules about your garden. The Calendar’s month drops its grid lines, and the year shows each month as dots, last year’s faint beside them. On the Log, the months wait for a tap on the garden picture, and the seasons line shows once you have more than one season.

    Try it ›

  • The reading pages get lighter: one row of links on top, and each claim a row you open

    November 9, 2026 · This site

    Content pages open sooner on a phone.

    The section links sit in one row under the header, and the way to the Spanish page rides beside the breadcrumb; on a Spanish page, the machine-translation notice is one line under the title, with the way back to English beside it. On guides, plant teams and explainers, each graded claim is one line with its grade beside it (a claim that doesn’t hold up keeps its card open); tap it for how it works, the evidence and the sources, exactly as before. A page’s citation, its link into the planner and how we grade now sit together under “About this page”, at the end of “More ways Milpa can help”. Help’s questions open one at a time, and What’s new leads with its headlines.

    Try it ›

  • Every frost page with a date draws its season, and says in one line how sure it is

    November 9, 2026 · This site

    Every frost page with a date now opens on a small drawing of its own numbers.

    The growing season is a band between the two median dates, with the 1-in-5 and 1-in-10 ticks at each end and both tails in words, and the months’ highs and lows stand as a strip over their rows. Where the dates come from is one plain line: the station or the weather model, how far, how many winters, and the grade word; tap it for every word that was there before. The same line sits on the Calendar’s climate card and on Answers.

    Try it ›

  • Summer’s colors warm up

    November 9, 2026 · The whole app

    The summer look’s accent is now a ripe-tomato red in place of the sky blue, which sat too close to winter’s slate.

    The sunflower and the sunlit cream stay. Pick a season under Display on Account to see it.

    Try it ›

  • Less said at once on the Calendar, Home and the Log

    November 8, 2026 · The whole app

    Each job on the Calendar is now two lines: what to do, then its date, its bed and how late it is.

    The how, the plant’s page, the reason for the date and “Not this year” sit behind one How button, which stands open on its own only until you’ve done that kind of job once in your garden. Explanations that ran long now fold behind their first line; tap the line to read the rest, or set Explanations to open on Account to keep them all open. Rename, close and delete sit behind the garden’s “…” on the Log.

    Try it ›

  • Quieter labels, the same words

    November 7, 2026 · The whole app

    The small labels over each part of a screen (the date, a section’s name, what a job is for) now read in the same upright serif as the headings, in sentence case, and dates and counts sit as plain figures.

    Nothing moved and no word changed; the screen says the same thing in a softer voice. In dark mode the ground now carries a little of the season’s own color.

    Try it ›

  • Basil waits for warm ground too

    November 4, 2026 · Calendar

    Basil now goes out with your tomatoes, once your days average about 16 °C (60 °F) as well as after your last frost.

    In Melbourne that moves it from early August to late October.

    Try it ›

  • Warm crops wait for warm ground

    November 4, 2026 · Calendar

    Corn, beans, squash and tomatoes now wait until your days average about 16 °C (60 °F), as well as for your last frost.

    Where spring warms slowly, that’s later: in Melbourne corn moves from late July to late October. Your frost dates still show beside the new day, and a seed you start indoors moves with it.

    Try it ›

  • Clearer names for places, plants and seasons

    November 3, 2026 · The whole app

    When the example garden isn’t at your place, Home now says where it is.

    Arrive from a plant’s page and the example opens with that plant marked on its season chart. A bed on the Plan has a Rename button. South of the equator a season carries both its years, like 2026–27, because it runs across the new year. Home says “sow more” only once you’ve sown that crop in that bed, and signing out keeps the language you were reading in.

    Try it ›

  • One planting day for a tomato, wherever you look

    November 3, 2026 · Calendar

    For tomatoes, peppers, melons and other crops that need warm days, the Calendar and the Dates page now give the same day to set them out, about two weeks after your usual last frost.

    Where frost after that day still comes in more than 1 year in 10, both say the safer date beside it. The page you reach from Dates is now called Dates too.

    Try it ›

  • The example garden, at your place

    November 2, 2026 · The whole app

    Look up your town on Dates and the example garden is built there, with its own dates.

    Its plant team is the one the planner picks for a 10 by 10 ft bed in the ground, sown on the days your frost dates give, or in your warm months where frost is rare, and last year’s diary keeps only the lines true for your climate. A plant or plant team you came from is planted in it when Milpa can date it there, and the example says why when it can’t. After you sign up, your first garden can start from it, with the same bed and team.

    Try it ›

  • Gardens without an account have ended

    November 2, 2026 · The whole app

    Gardens made on a device without an account ended on November 2, 2026.

    A device that still has one shows a line naming it, and one tap downloads it whole, with its beds, seasons and photos. Nothing is deleted. Your garden now lives in your free account, backed up and on every device you use.

  • One way in to your garden

    November 1, 2026 · The whole app

    Signed out, the menus now say Dates and Your garden.

    Plan, Calendar and Log had all led to the same sign-up. Your garden shows what a free account keeps, and its bed, calendar and diary each open the example garden at that screen so you can try them first. It names the plant or plant team you came from. Signed in, nothing changes.

    Try it ›

  • Your garden now lives in a free account

    November 1, 2026 · Plan

    The advice stays open to everyone: planting dates, frost odds, plant pages and guides need no account.

    A garden, its diary and its calendar now start with a free account, so your garden is backed up and on every device you use. The town you gave before signing up becomes your first garden’s place, even if the confirmation email opens in another browser. A garden made on a device without an account could be used until November 2, 2026; since then one line downloads it whole. This replaces October’s “A diary with no account”.

    Try it ›

October 2026

  • Mexico’s words, and basil with the tomatoes

    October 31, 2026 · Plan

    A garden in Mexico now says chícharo, betabel and calabacita, a pot keeps only the depth you give it, and the Spanish app no longer slips into English on the map or in Design it yourself.

    Basil goes out with the tomatoes, about two weeks after your usual last frost, and rosemary in a zone too cold for its winter says to keep it in a pot indoors.

    Try it ›

  • Any season to keep, and a reason per crop

    October 31, 2026 · Log

    Export any season from the Log, or all of them in one zip.

    Closing a season asks how each crop ended, so a disease on the tomatoes is never written down for the beans. A new pot or bed never lands on top of another one, the plants you pick in Design it yourself are kept as you pick them, and tomatoes, peppers and basil are picked until your fall frost.

    Try it ›

  • A plan made in autumn keeps for spring

    October 31, 2026 · Calendar

    Plan your beds after the first frost and the plan carries into the new year, on the Calendar and Home.

    A February sowing typed in the Log can start the new season in one tap.

    Try it ›

  • Renaming a bed renames it

    October 31, 2026 · Plan

    Give a bed a new name in the Plan and it keeps its plans and its place.

    Before, saving left a copy under the old name on the same spot, so the garden counted one bed too many.

    Try it ›

  • The front page starts with your planting dates

    October 30, 2026 · The whole app

    Type your town or ZIP code on the front page and you go straight to your planting dates.

    Further down, the evidence opens with a pair of watering answers: one the studies back, and one old tip they rule out.

    Try it ›
