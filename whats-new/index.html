November 2026

  • Your week, just under its ring

    November 22, 2026 · Home

    On Home and the Calendar the year ring stays on its green band, and your week’s words and buttons now sit just under it, on the page.

    On a wide screen the Calendar’s band runs edge to edge, with your climate notes beside the week under it.

    Try it ›

  • The green band holds a picture, never paragraphs

    November 22, 2026 · This site

    The dark green band now holds only a page’s one picture: a year ring, a row of photos, a map, or a plant team’s own photo or planting plan.

    The words that stood on it, from About and Help to a rule’s advice and a frost page’s odds, are back on the page, and no page has more than one band.

    Try it ›

  • Counts said plainly, with photos

    November 21, 2026 · This site

    The wound lines that counted things on the libraries and the evidence pages are gone, with their “How to read this”.

    Each library’s band now shows photos instead: the plants the most rules apply to, claims about plants, a guide from each shelf, and every plant team with a photo of its own, each a link. Its numbers sit under the photos as a plain list. Pages that count with nothing to picture show their numbers as large figures, the frost home shows the coverage map, and each rule and folklore page says in one line how many sources it cites and how each is dated.

    Try it ›

  • One green band, the same all year

    November 21, 2026 · This site

    The dark green band across the site’s pages and the app is now one sage green in every season and in dark mode too, and on a wide screen it runs from edge to edge on most pages.

    On a ring, the stretch with no frost is drawn in pale cream so it stands clear of the band, and each crop’s line takes a color that never matches it.

    Try it ›

  • A second photo for the plants the guides start from, and plant teams in photos

    November 21, 2026 · Plants

    The plants that open a growing guide now show a second photo of what the first one doesn’t: tomato and pepper flowers, corn’s silks, an onion swelling in the ground.

    Several plant teams have a photo of their own, from the Three Sisters on a mound to ducks in a rice paddy, and a plant’s page shows its teams with their photos. In the app, a plant’s photo beside its dates, in the Plan and in the Log now opens its card.

    Try it ›

  • Photos in the guides, by family, and on every frost page

    November 20, 2026 · This site

    Many more guides now open a section with a photo of the job it describes, from drip lines to hardening off.

    A few guides got brand-new photos too: a tomato going into the ground, marigolds among the vegetables, raised beds on wet ground, and seed trays of tomato and pepper. The plants list shows most of its families as rows of photos. On a plant page, rule or plant team, “Read next” is now a row of pictured guides. Each place’s frost page now suggests guides of its own, picked to fit its season.

    Try it ›

  • Rule, folklore, frost and guide pages now show photos

    November 20, 2026 · This site

    A rule’s page now pictures the plants it applies to, and each photo takes you to that plant’s page.

    A folklore page shows the plants its claim names and the guides that look into it. Under a place’s frost dates you’ll find guides to read next, each opening at the part that matters for those dates. A guide shows the plants it covers as photos too. Every row has its photo credits one tap below it. And every plant now has a photo of its own, the artichoke, the lemon and the avocado among the newest.

    Try it ›

  • Photos are back on plant team pages, the plants list and the front page

    November 19, 2026 · Plants

    A plant team’s page now shows its plants as a row of photos, and each photo takes you to that plant’s page.

    The plants list opens with a row of plants to start from, the guides list shows each guide’s photo on a phone, and the front page has its shelf of growing guides again. On a phone a row swipes sideways, and on a wide screen it’s a grid. More plants have a photo of their own, calendula and quinoa among them. Under every photo, the license now links to the license itself and the source links to the photo’s own page. A new Photo credits page lists every photo with its author.

    Try it ›

  • Home and the Calendar draw your garden’s year

    November 18, 2026 · The whole app

    Home now opens with your garden’s year drawn as a ring.

    It shows when frost is likely where you are, the season with no frost, and a line for each crop from the day it went in to its first picking. A sowing you’ve logged is a solid line and one that’s planned is dashed. Tap a crop’s name under the ring to pick out its line. Your photo, what’s ready to pick and this week’s job follow, and each crop’s standing is still said in words below them. On the Calendar, the Year view is the same ring, with last year’s record fainter inside it and a row for each month. The week and the month each open with their stretch of the year laid flat. In the US the ring now draws the fall frost odds as well as spring’s, from the same weather station.

    Try it ›

  • The front page draws a garden’s year

    November 17, 2026 · The whole app

    The front page now opens with a year drawn as a ring.

    It is an example garden in Albany, New York: when frost is likely there, the season with no frost, and when corn, beans, squash and a tomato go in and are first picked. The dates are the ones Milpa gives for that place, and the page says where they come from. Type your own town or ZIP code beside it and you get your dates. Further down, the questions are plain rows under one line of the year, and one section says what is open to everyone and what a free account adds. The page says it in about 500 words where it took 900. The plant teams, the guides and the frost odds it used to sample are one tap away in their own sections.

    Try it ›

  • Plant and team pages draw the year where you live

    November 17, 2026 · This site

    Look up your ZIP code or city once on the frost page.

    After that, a plant’s page draws its year for your place as a ring: when to start it indoors, when to sow it or set it out, and about when its first picking comes. They are the same dates “When do I plant this?” gives you. A plant team’s page shows a small ring with each crop’s planting day, and a guide about one plant draws that plant’s year. Your place is kept on your device and nothing is sent anywhere. “Can I still sow this?” now draws your place’s year too, with today marked on it. Long plant and team pages keep their extra sections one tap away, and no word was cut. Rows, buttons and folds also move a little under a pointer, and stay still if your device asks for reduced motion.

    Try it ›

  • The libraries count themselves in one drawn line

    November 17, 2026 · This site

    The rules, the folklore, the guides, the plants, the plant teams, the sources and the frost places are each drawn as one line wound from the middle out.

    Each group is a run of the line, as long as its count. With every line you’ll find the numbers it draws, and “How to read this” explains it. Long pages now keep one dark band for their main point, and a long list shows its first rows with the rest one tap away. Nothing was cut: every word is still on its page.

    Try it ›

  • Frost-rare places draw their year too, and a frost page’s ring stands on a dark band

    November 16, 2026 · This site

    Where a frost page says frost is rare, it now draws the year as a ring that is green all the way round, and says in one line whether the record holds no frost at all or only in fewer than half of years.

    A page that says frost comes in some or most winters draws no ring, because its record has no frost in a middle year and the ring would say the opposite. Where a frost page draws its year, the ring and the dates beneath it now stand together on one dark band. Each month’s numbers fold under the drawing of the months, and nearby places are rows you tap.

    Try it ›

  • Every frost page with a date now draws its year as a ring

    November 16, 2026 · This site

    Each one draws the whole year as one ring, where the small season line was.

    The green stroke is the season with no frost. The fine marks show when frost is likely, and more marks mean it’s more likely. Each date on the ring also stands in the lists underneath, and “How to read this” explains every mark. Look up a ZIP code or a city on the frost page and you’ll get the same ring for that place.

    Try it ›

  • The Plan’s plant list gets shorter

    November 10, 2026 · The whole app

    Beside a plant team’s drawing, each kind of plant is now one line: its colored dot, its name and how many to plant.

    Where they go and how far apart are said once, above the lines that share them. “Design it yourself” keeps its full sentences.

    Try it ›

  • The app says less at once, and keeps the rest a tap away

    November 9, 2026 · The whole app

    Evidence opens on the folklore, or on your own garden’s rules, with the tabs and search behind “Browse everything”.

    Account’s settings lose their boxes. On the Plan, a team’s card shows its size and kind once you open it, and its note on how the plants get along folds behind its first line once something is planted. Dates keeps your own crops and the quick picks as chips and folds the rest by kind, and a plant’s page leads with the rules about your garden. The Calendar’s month drops its grid lines, and the year shows each month as dots, last year’s faint beside them. On the Log, the months wait for a tap on the garden picture, and the seasons line shows once you have more than one season.

    Try it ›

  • The reading pages get lighter: one row of links on top, and each claim a row you open

    November 9, 2026 · This site

    Content pages open sooner on a phone.

    The section links sit in one row under the header, and the way to the Spanish page rides beside the breadcrumb; on a Spanish page, the machine-translation notice is one line under the title, with the way back to English beside it. On guides, plant teams and explainers, each graded claim is one line with its grade beside it (a claim that doesn’t hold up keeps its card open); tap it for how it works, the evidence and the sources, exactly as before. A page’s citation, its link into the planner and how we grade now sit together under “About this page”, at the end of “More ways Milpa can help”. Help’s questions open one at a time, and What’s new leads with its headlines.

    Try it ›

  • Every frost page with a date draws its season, and says in one line how sure it is

    November 9, 2026 · This site

    Every frost page with a date now opens on a small drawing of its own numbers.

    The growing season is a band between the two median dates, with the 1-in-5 and 1-in-10 ticks at each end and both tails in words, and the months’ highs and lows stand as a strip over their rows. Where the dates come from is one plain line: the station or the weather model, how far, how many winters, and the grade word; tap it for every word that was there before. The same line sits on the Calendar’s climate card and on Answers.

    Try it ›

  • Summer’s colors warm up

    November 9, 2026 · The whole app

    The summer look’s accent is now a ripe-tomato red in place of the sky blue, which sat too close to winter’s slate.

    The sunflower and the sunlit cream stay. Pick a season under Display on Account to see it.

    Try it ›

  • Less said at once on the Calendar, Home and the Log

    November 8, 2026 · The whole app

    Each job on the Calendar is now two lines: what to do, then its date, its bed and how late it is.

    The how, the plant’s page, the reason for the date and “Not this year” sit behind one How button, which stands open on its own only until you’ve done that kind of job once in your garden. Explanations that ran long now fold behind their first line; tap the line to read the rest, or set Explanations to open on Account to keep them all open. Rename, close and delete sit behind the garden’s “…” on the Log.

    Try it ›

  • Quieter labels, the same words

    November 7, 2026 · The whole app

    The small labels over each part of a screen (the date, a section’s name, what a job is for) now read in the same upright serif as the headings, in sentence case, and dates and counts sit as plain figures.

    Nothing moved and no word changed; the screen says the same thing in a softer voice. In dark mode the ground now carries a little of the season’s own color.

    Try it ›
