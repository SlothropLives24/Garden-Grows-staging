  • Frost dates from the weather square on your own ground

    October 20, 2026 · The whole app

  • Where summers stay cool, warm crops come with a heads-up

    October 19, 2026 · Calendar

    A frost-free season isn’t always a warm one. Read more

    Glasgow has more than six months between frosts, but no month there averages the 18 °C (65 °F) or so that okra, tomatoes and peppers grow in. Where frost sets your season and too few months are warm enough for a crop, What grows here now lists it as fitting with care, and the Calendar, your bed’s card and its plant team say why: no month is warm enough, or only July and August are. They suggest cover, an early start or a quick variety. Planting dates stay as they were. Where no month is warm enough, the Calendar no longer promises a picking date, and where only a few are, that date is marked approximate. Later in the season, once a new planting would ripen in months cooler than a warm crop likes, its planting job and your bed’s card say so, even while there’s still time before the frost: in Santiago, tomatoes from January 30. The site’s Can I still sow this? tool now says the same beside each crop. Where frost is rare and you’ve given your own last frost date, your plans still follow each month’s warmth, and only frost-tender crops wait for your date. And where we can’t check a place’s monthly temperatures, What grows here no longer counts luffa, basil and other warm crops with no temperature range on record as fitting.

  • Cabbage, kale and lettuce get their own planting window

    October 18, 2026 · Calendar

  • Soil tests that work wherever you garden

    October 18, 2026 · Log

    Labs measure soil pH in more than one way, and the pH ranges in Milpa are all written for a pH measured in water. Read more

    So when you enter a lab’s pH on the soil card, it asks which pH your report gives. A pH in water is checked against your plants. A pH in calcium chloride or another salt solution is saved and shown with how it was measured, and no plant is checked against it, because the gap between the two changes from one soil to the next. A lab pH you saved before this change waits for your answer, and a home kit reading is checked just as it was. The soil cautions read in Spanish in the Spanish app, the soil-test guide’s title and opening and the soil card’s ladder drop their prices, and logging compost leaves your pH reading standing.
