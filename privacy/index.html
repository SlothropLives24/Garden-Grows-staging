Your data and your privacy

Milpa keeps as little about you as a garden planner can. Signed out, everything stays on your device, unless you choose to email us a note. Signed in, your gardens sync to your own account and nobody else's. Your plans and your photos are never sold, never used to advertise to you, and never mixed into the public plant data.

Last updated October 28, 2026. Data controller: Milpa Gardens.

This page says what is collected, who it is sent to, and what you can do about it. Every line is true of what the app actually does; when a data flow changes, this page changes with it.

What Milpa collects, and only when

Your email address
Your account identity, used to sign you in and to show teammates who made a change on a garden you share with them. Collected only if you create an account.
Your garden's location
The address you type and its approximate coordinates, so the planner can find your frost dates, hardiness zone and daylength. Collected only when you enter it.
Your garden itself
Beds, plantings, seasons, notes, soil observations, any plants you add, and your own frost dates if you enter them. This is the work you do in the app.
Team messages
If you share a garden with other people, the posts you write to that garden's team. Only members of that garden can see them.
A note you email us
If you tap "Wrong for your garden? Tell us" under your frost dates, your own mail app opens a message to us with the frost dates on your screen already written in, and where they come from: the weather station or the weather model's square, with how far away it is. From Answers it also carries the ZIP code or town you gave there, if you gave one. From the Calendar it carries no address; add your town if you like. Nothing is sent until you send it, and you can change or delete any of it first. We read it to put the dates right, and it is never added to your account.

What never leaves your device

Photos
Every photo you attach stays in this browser and is never uploaded. The export is how you move or back them up.
Everything, when signed out
With no account, everything above lives only on this device, including the Log of what you plant, and nothing leaves your browser. The one exception is a note you choose to email us, which leaves from your own mail app when you send it. That also means nothing backs it up but you: the season export on the Log is your own copy, and the daily backup comes with a free account. Signing in carries what is on this device to your account.
A sentence you type or speak into the Log
The Log reads a plain sentence ("planted 12 tomatoes in bed 3", "frost last night") into the entry it means and shows it back to you to check before anything is saved. That reading happens on this device, from word lists and the plant names built into the app. No service reads your words, and nothing is sent anywhere for it. A sentence it can't read is kept as the note you wrote.
Your plant list, for the site's search page
So the search page can offer "Your plants", the planner keeps a short list of which plants are in your garden (just which plants, with no locations, notes or dates) in this browser. The search page reads it there, and it is never sent anywhere. Clearing this site's data removes it.
Your reminders
Your week and its heads-ups are worked out on this device from your garden, and the list your phone reads for its reminders stays in this browser. Turning reminders on asks your browser's permission and sends nothing to a server. Which notices you have read in the app's bell is kept in this browser too, for 30 days. When you're signed in, how often you're reminded and the day “Your week” comes sync with your other display settings, such as your units.
Which side of the equator you garden on
The app and this site change their colors with the seasons. So that you see your own season, they keep one letter in this browser, n or s: whether your garden is north or south of the equator, or, until you place a garden, the last ZIP code or town you looked up, with a note saying it came from a lookup so a garden's letter is never changed by one. The place itself is not kept for this, and neither is ever sent anywhere. Clearing this site's data removes both.

Who your data is sent to

Only these services, only for what each line says.

Supabase (our backend host)
When you sign in and sync, your account data is stored on Supabase. It is readable only by your own account (and by teammates for a garden you explicitly share). We do not run ads on it and it is never sold.
Google, the US Census Bureau, and OpenStreetMap
When you look up an address or a town, the text you type is sent to these services to turn it into a location. Only what you typed is sent. Your plans never are.
Google and Esri
When you view the satellite map, the app requests map imagery for the area you are looking at. That reveals roughly where you are looking to the imagery provider.
GoatCounter (visitor counts)
Page visits are counted anonymously, and so are a few steps a visit takes, like which of the front page's doors it opens. There are no cookies, no accounts and no personal data. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control are honored, and your plants and plans are never part of any count.

Like any request your browser makes, each of these carries your device's IP address to the service it reaches. That is how the web works, so we mention it.

How it is stored

Your account data is protected at the database by a rule that lets each account read and write only its own rows, so one account has no way to reach another's garden. The plant knowledge that powers the app is a public file built into the app itself; your data never mixes into it. Once a day we save an encrypted copy of the account database, so a fault on our side can't lose your garden. The copy is locked with a key only Milpa holds, kept in private storage at our code host (GitHub, which can't read it), and deleted after 30 days.

Your control

Delete it

Delete your account from the Account page and it removes your account and every record it holds (your gardens, plans, seasons and team messages) in one step, including messages you wrote on gardens others shared with you and any pending invitations addressed to you. You can also delete a single garden on its own, or a single entry in the Log: its date and words leave every device you sync, and the season keeps only a marker (the entry's id and when you deleted it) so an older copy can't bring it back. An entry written before the Log could delete entries (October 2026) has an id made from its own date and words, so for one of those the marker can reveal them to anyone who can read the season. A soil pH reading you remove from the soil card leaves every device you sync the same way; its marker keeps the reading's ground, date and kind, never its pH. Copies already in those daily backups are deleted within 30 days.

Take it with you

Export any season as a plain-text file, with its photos in a matching zip. Your data is yours to keep and to move.

What we never do

  • Sell your data, or share it to advertise to you.
  • Build a profile of you, or track you across other sites.
  • Put your plants, plans or photos into any analytics.
  • Mix your data into the public plant corpus.

Contact

Questions, or want your data removed and cannot reach the Account page? Write to us. This page is versioned in the open with the rest of the project, so what it promised and when is a matter of record. privacy@milpa.garden
