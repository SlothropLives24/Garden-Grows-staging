One you might not Thyme · Cabbage

Brassicas with aromatic herbs

Plant strong-smelling herbs among your cabbages and you may see less caterpillar damage. One university trial found that with thyme. Nobody has shown exactly why, and plenty of trials find nothing at all.

A good hunch See the evidence ›
Under the soil Carrots · Onions · Dill

Carrots, onions and dill

Onions planted through the carrots throw the carrot rust fly off the scent, so fewer maggots reach the roots. A dill border gives you something to cut for the table.

A good hunch See the evidence ›
At the edge of the bed Hubbard squash · Cucumber

The hubbard ring

Circle your cucumbers with hubbard squash. The beetles much prefer it, so they crowd onto the ring and mostly leave the cucumbers, and the wilt they carry, alone. You still clear them off. The ring just gathers them.

          Where the plant numbers come from

          Every number a plant contributes, like how wide it grows, how tall, or how long to harvest, carries a confidence mark. A number marked estimated is one nobody has confirmed against a published source yet. Use it for laying out a bed, but don't quote it. The app tracks which, and here they are.

          loading…

          The tree sizes were checked against land-grant extension publications in July 2026: UGA Extension Circular 740 (Home Garden Apples) and Circular 1063 (Home Garden Peaches), University of Maine Bulletin #2034 (Plum Production in Maine), University of California ANR's plum rootstock guidance, and University of Missouri Extension G6030 (peach training and pruning). Where a size is derived from a published band instead of quoted from it, the number stays marked estimated. Dwarf apples, for instance, are "about 30 to 40 percent as large as standard trees", with no stated height. There is no dwarf pear in this corpus because extension sources say a home grower cannot buy one.

          Garden circles

          A garden circle lets more than one person tend the same garden. Create one, invite the people who help, and you'll all see the same beds, seasons, and diary.

          Everyone you add to a circle can see that circle's gardens, including their location on the map and their full seasons. Everyone in a circle can also see each other's sign-in email. Your photos and your display settings never travel.

          Share a garden with a circle and everyone in it sees its beds, seasons, and diary, and can tend it with you. Only the garden's owner can share or remove it.

          Privacy Policy
          Read how your data is kept

          Signed out, your garden data lives only on this device, and nothing leaves your browser unless you choose to email us a note. Nothing backs it up, either: the season export on the Log is your own copy, and the daily backup comes with a free account.

          Signed in, your addresses, growing areas and seasons are stored in your account so they can follow you across devices. They are readable only by your own account, and deleting your account above removes them in one tap.

          The plant knowledge that powers the app is a public file built into the app itself. Your data never mixes into it.

          Address lookup and map imagery are fetched only when you use them, and those requests carry only what you typed or the area you are viewing. They never carry your plans. Visits are counted anonymously, with no cookies and no personal data, and Do Not Track is honored. Your plants and your plans are never part of any analytics.

          Delete account

          Removes your account and every record synced to it. Its copy on this device goes too, with the photos in its gardens. Gardens you made without signing in stay here. Export any season you want to keep first.
