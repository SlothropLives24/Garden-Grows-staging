      Why trust this?

      Every recommendation in this app cites a rule. Every rule states its mechanism, its evidence grade, and the sources it stands on. Here is all of it - including the folklore we checked and won't repeat.

      Where the climate numbers come from

      Your frost window is precomputed from NOAA NCEI 1991–2020 station normals (tier A, measured), a PRISM terrain offset where you're between stations (tier B, modelled), or ERA5 reanalysis where no station is in range (tier C). The app always shows which one you got and how far the data travelled. After three logged seasons, your own ground's observations supersede all of it.

      Where the plant numbers come from

      Every number a plant contributes - how wide it grows, how tall, how long to harvest - carries a confidence mark. A number marked estimated is one nobody has confirmed against a published source yet: usable for laying out a bed, not something to quote. The app tracks which, and here they are.

      The tree sizes were checked against land-grant extension publications in July 2026: UGA Extension Circular 740 (Home Garden Apples) and Circular 1063 (Home Garden Peaches), University of Maine Bulletin #2034 (Plum Production in Maine), University of California ANR's plum rootstock guidance, and University of Missouri Extension G6030 (peach training and pruning). Where a size is derived from a published band rather than quoted from it - dwarf apples, for instance, are "about 30 to 40 percent as large as standard trees" rather than a stated height - the number stays marked estimated. There is no dwarf pear in this corpus because extension sources say a home grower cannot buy one.
