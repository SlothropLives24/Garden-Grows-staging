Growing summer squash, where a few plants are plenty

The most over-planted vegetable in the garden. Why a row of zucchini buries a household, what bush-or-vining changes about its space, and why the first flowers fall off.

What you need

  • Restraint, because one or two plants per person is plenty and a row feeds the neighborhood against its will
  • A full meter3 feet of room per bush plant, more for a vining type, or a sturdy trellis
  • A knife for harvesting at hand-length, every day or two once they start

Summer squash is the easiest serious-yield crop in this library, and that’s its danger. A healthy zucchini keeps setting fruit all summer, and a whole row produces faster than any household eats. Plant 1–2 per person, and spend the ground you saved on something that doesn’t multiply overnight.

How much room does a summer squash need?

Squash is the crop the bush-or-vining guide was written about, and the short version bears repeating. The same species is either a bush, a big rosette holding about a square meter10 square feet, or a vining type that runs 2–4 m7–13 ft and swallows a small bed whole. Most zucchini are bush types, and many heirloom summer squashes vine. In a small bed, a vining type needs either a direction to run or a trellis.

A trellised squash is a real option, with the same payoff as a trellised cucumber: a fraction of the footprint, cleaner fruit, drier leaves. The planner marks vining squash better with a support, and the staking guide sizes that support. A fruiting squash vine is heavy, so use a cattle panel or a sturdy frame. Netting won’t hold it.

My squash has flowers but no fruit. Should I panic?

Sow direct after frost into warmed soil. Squash is tender, fast (45–60 days to first fruit for a bush type, 50–65 for a vining one), and does badly when transplanted. Then comes the moment that generates more worried questions than any squash disease: the first flowers fall off without fruiting. Squash opens its male flowers first, often for a week or more, and only the females, with a tiny squash already visible behind the bloom, become fruit. Early drop is normal. When flowers of both kinds are open and fruit still isn’t setting, the problem is pollination, because insects have to carry the pollen. The pollination guide picks it up from there.

When and how often should I harvest?

Most of what zucchini are famous for going wrong comes down to harvesting habit. Pick at hand-length, every day or two. The fruit is at its best at that size, and a single marrow left to swell to forearm size tells the plant to slow down. In high season this is a daily walk, which is what “easy to grow, demanding to keep up with” means.

Squash is a cucurbit like cucumber and melon, so it carries the same rotation load. When you log where it grew, the planner keeps the family moving off ground it recently held. And if you’re tempted by the oldest squash arrangement of all, the Three Sisters mound gives it the ground-cover role it’s held for a few thousand years.
