Watering with hydrogen peroxide oxygenates the roots and cures root rot.

Contested

Hydrogen peroxide won't oxygenate your roots in any lasting way. It breaks down in soil within hours, and it can harm the helpful life in the soil. It can kill some fungi, but root rot comes from waterlogged soil, and peroxide doesn't change that. Fix the drainage and ease off the watering.

What is actually known

Hydrogen peroxide does break down into water and oxygen, which is where the idea comes from, but it is no lasting supply to the roots. In soils it breaks down within hours (measured half-lives run from about one to seven hours), fastest where there is more organic matter. It is not harmless to the soil either. It can damage or destroy soil organisms, and large treatments can harm mycorrhizal fungi. Peroxide is a real broad-spectrum fungicide, used as a soil drench, but it does not fix what causes root rot. The water molds behind it live in practically all cultivated soils and kill roots when poor drainage or overwatering keeps the soil wet. Good drainage and water management are what control it.

How we grade evidence
