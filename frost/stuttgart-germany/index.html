Frost dates for Stuttgart, Germany

Half of years have their last spring frost by March 31, but 1 year in 10 still frosts after April 19.

These frost dates are the Stuttgart (Neckartal) weather station’s own, less than 2 km (about a mile) from Stuttgart, from 22 years of its daily lows in NOAA’s GHCN-Daily records. It recorded a frost in 22 of its 22 winters on record from 1991 to 2020. Hollows and low ground near your garden can frost later in spring and earlier in fall than the station does.

The weather model’s dates for Stuttgart: last frost around April 16.

A weather station’s own frost dates, graded Promising. Its dates come from the Stuttgart (Neckartal) weather station, less than 2 km (about a mile) away, with 22 complete years of daily lows.

If you know your own frost dates, enter them under your place on the Plan, and your plans go by yours, with these shown beside them. See where every city’s dates come from on the coverage map.

The frost dates are a weather station’s. They come from NOAA’s GHCN-Daily daily records for the Stuttgart (Neckartal) station (GME00129118), less than 2 km (about a mile) from Stuttgart: Milpa read its daily lows for 1991 to 2020 and, in each of the 22 years it recorded in full, the last spring night and the first fall night at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather station measures one spot, so the ground around it can differ: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same dates for a garden here and grades them Promising.

The frost count is a weather station’s. It comes from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network daily records (GHCN-Daily) for the Stuttgart (Neckartal) station (GME00129118), less than 2 km (about a mile) from Stuttgart. A winter counts when the station recorded its three coldest months, and it is a frost winter when a night in it fell to 0 °C (32 °F) or below.

The weather model, for comparison. Its frost dates for Stuttgart come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo: its daily lows for the point at 48.75, 9.25, 5 km (3 miles) from the center of Stuttgart.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.

The highs and lows here are the 1991 to 2020 normals of WMO weather station 00010739, 5 km (3 miles) from Stuttgart at 314 m (1,030 ft) above sea level, not the weather model’s.
