Frost dates for Amsterdam, Netherlands

Half of years have their last spring frost by April 6, but 1 year in 10 still frosts after April 23.

These frost dates are the Schiphol weather station’s own, 9 km (6 miles) from Amsterdam, from 30 years of its daily lows in NOAA’s GHCN-Daily records. It recorded a frost in 29 of its 29 winters on record from 1991 to 2020. Hollows and low ground near your garden can frost later in spring and earlier in fall than the station does.

The weather model’s dates for Amsterdam: last frost around March 23.

From the Schiphol weather station, 9 km (6 miles) away, 30 winters · Promising

A weather station’s own frost dates, graded Promising. Its dates come from the Schiphol weather station, 9 km (6 miles) away, with 30 complete years of daily lows.

If you know your own frost dates, enter them under your place on the Plan, and your plans go by yours, with these shown beside them. See where every city’s dates come from on the coverage map.

The frost dates are a weather station’s. They come from NOAA’s GHCN-Daily daily records for the Schiphol station (NLE00152485), 9 km (6 miles) from Amsterdam: Milpa read its daily lows for 1991 to 2020 and, in each of the 30 years it recorded in full, the last spring night and the first fall night at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather station measures one spot, so the ground around it can differ: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same dates for a garden here and grades them Promising.

The frost count is a weather station’s. It comes from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network daily records (GHCN-Daily) for the Schiphol station (NLE00152485), 9 km (6 miles) from Amsterdam. A winter counts when the station recorded its three coldest months, and it is a frost winter when a night in it fell to 0 °C (32 °F) or below.

The weather model, for comparison. Its frost dates for Amsterdam come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo: its daily lows for the point at 52.25, 4.75, 16 km (10 miles) from the center of Amsterdam.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.

The highs and lows here are the 1991 to 2020 normals of WMO weather station 0-20000-0-06215, 40 km (25 miles) from Amsterdam at -1 m (-3 ft) above sea level, not the weather model’s.
