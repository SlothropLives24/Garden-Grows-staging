Frost dates for Aleppo, Syria

Half of years have their last spring frost by February 22, but 1 year in 10 still frosts after March 20.

A frost date is really a percentile, here from 30 years (1991 to 2020) of a weather model’s daily lows near Aleppo: in about half of years a frost still comes after the median date. Both numbers are below, so you can choose the risk yourself.

Where these numbers come from

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at 36.25, 37.25, 8 km (5 miles) from the center of Aleppo. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, under CC BY 4.0. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information, 2026.

Dry months from CHIRPS (Climate Hazards Center, UC Santa Barbara), in the public domain, checked against GPCC (Deutscher Wetterdienst), under CC BY 4.0.

The highs and lows here are the 1991 to 2020 normals of WMO weather station 0-20000-0-40007, 9 km (6 miles) from Aleppo at 393 m (1,289 ft) above sea level, not the weather model’s.

Highs and lows from the WMO Climatological Standard Normals for 1991-2020 (WMO Member Nations, 2023), published by the US National Centers for Environmental Information.
