From a weather model, not a station; no long record nearby to check it against · A good hunch

The weather model alone, graded A good hunch. Its dates come from the weather model’s square centered 23 km (14 miles) away.

If you know your own frost dates, enter them under your place on the Plan, and your plans go by yours, with these shown beside them. See where every city’s dates come from on the coverage map.

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at 37.25, 126.75, 23 km (14 miles) from the center of Suwon. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

The model’s squares near Suwon stand at different heights. This page reads the one whose ground stands at 13 m (43 ft), the closest to the city’s own height of 35 m (115 ft). The nearer square, 21 km (13 miles) from the center, stands at 162 m (531 ft). Height only picks the square. It never changes a temperature.

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.

The highs and lows here are the 1991 to 2020 normals of WMO weather station 00047112, 42 km (26 miles) from Suwon at 68 m (223 ft) above sea level, not the weather model’s.
