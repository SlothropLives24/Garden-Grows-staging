Frost dates for Shenzhen, China

Frost is rare here. In 30 years of a weather model’s daily lows, not one night reached freezing, so the growing season runs all year.

How sure is this answer?

The weather model alone, graded A good hunch. Whether frost comes here, and how often, is read from the weather model’s square centered 26 km (16 miles) away. The nearest station with dates of its own, Guangzhou, is 98 km (61 miles) away, too far to stand in.

Where these numbers come from

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at 22.75, 114.25, 26 km (16 miles) from the center of Shenzhen. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, under CC BY 4.0. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information, 2026.

Dry months from CHIRPS (Climate Hazards Center, UC Santa Barbara), in the public domain, checked against GPCC (Deutscher Wetterdienst), under CC BY 4.0.

The highs and lows here are the 1991 to 2020 normals of WMO weather station 0-20000-0-45005, 29 km (18 miles) from Shenzhen at 32 m (105 ft) above sea level, not the weather model’s.

Highs and lows from the WMO Climatological Standard Normals for 1991-2020 (WMO Member Nations, 2023), published by the US National Centers for Environmental Information.
