Frost dates for Jabalpur, India

Frost is rare here. The Jabalpur weather station, 3 km (2 miles) from Jabalpur, recorded no frost in its 15 winters on record from 1991 to 2020, so the growing season runs all year. In hills, inland valleys and suburbs away from the coast, frost can still come, more often than at the station.

Where these numbers come from

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at 23.25, 79.75, 22 km (14 miles) from the center of Jabalpur. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

The frost count is a weather station’s. It comes from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network daily records (GHCN-Daily) for the Jabalpur station (IN011180800), 3 km (2 miles) from Jabalpur. A winter counts when the station recorded its three coldest months, and it is a frost winter when a night in it fell to 0 °C (32 °F) or below. Any frost dates on this page are the weather model’s.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.
