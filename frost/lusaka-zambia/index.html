Frost dates for Lusaka, Zambia

Frost is rare here. In 29 years of a weather model’s daily lows, not one night reached freezing, so the growing season runs all year. In hills, inland valleys and suburbs away from the coast, frost can still come in most winters: the model’s squares, about 25 km (16 miles) across, miss the coldest nights there.

Where these numbers come from

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at -15.25, 28.25, 19 km (12 miles) from the center of Lusaka. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.
