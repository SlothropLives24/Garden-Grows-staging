Frost dates for Birmingham, United Kingdom

Half of years have their last spring frost by April 16, but 1 year in 10 still frosts after April 30.

These frost dates are the Cet Central England weather station’s own, 9 km (6 miles) from Birmingham, from 30 years of its daily lows in NOAA’s GHCN-Daily records. It recorded a frost in 29 of its 29 winters on record from 1991 to 2020. Hollows and low ground near your garden can frost later in spring and earlier in fall than the station does.

The weather model’s dates for Birmingham: last frost around April 15.

A frost date is really a percentile, here from 30 years (1991 to 2020) of a weather station’s daily lows: in about half of years a frost still comes after the median date. Both numbers are below, so you can choose the risk yourself.

Where these numbers come from

The frost dates are a weather station’s. They come from NOAA’s GHCN-Daily daily records for the Cet Central England station (UK000000000), 9 km (6 miles) from Birmingham: Milpa read its daily lows for 1991 to 2020 and, in each of the 30 years it recorded in full, the last spring night and the first fall night at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

Frost counts and dates from GHCN-Daily, version 3.35-upd-2026101817, NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, cited as NCEI asks: Menne, Matthew J., Imke Durre, Bryant Korzeniewski, Shelley McNeill, Kristy Thomas, Xungang Yin, Steven Anthony, Ron Ray, Russell S. Vose, Byron E.Gleason, and Tamara G. Houston (2012): Global Historical Climatology Network - Daily (GHCN-Daily), Version 3. Daily minimum temperature (TMIN), stations outside the United States, 1991-2020. NOAA National Climatic Data Center. doi:10.7289/V5D21VHZ. Accessed 2026-10-20.

A weather station measures one spot, so the ground around it can differ: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same dates for a garden here and grades them Promising.

The frost count is a weather station’s. It comes from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network daily records (GHCN-Daily) for the Cet Central England station (UK000000000), 9 km (6 miles) from Birmingham. A winter counts when the station recorded its three coldest months, and it is a frost winter when a night in it fell to 0 °C (32 °F) or below.

The weather model, for comparison. Its frost dates for Birmingham come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo: its daily lows for the point at 52.25, -1.75, 28 km (17 miles) from the center of Birmingham.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.
