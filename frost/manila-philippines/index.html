Frost dates for Manila, Philippines

Frost is rare here. The Science Garden weather station, 5 km (3 miles) from Manila, recorded no frost in its 10 winters on record from 1991 to 2020, so the growing season runs all year.

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at 14.75, 121.25, 33 km (21 miles) from the center of Manila. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

The frost count is a weather station’s. It comes from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network daily records (GHCN-Daily) for the Science Garden station (RP000098430), 5 km (3 miles) from Manila. A winter counts when the station recorded its three coldest months, and it is a frost winter when a night in it fell to 0 °C (32 °F) or below. Any frost dates on this page are the weather model’s.

The highs and lows here are the 1991 to 2020 normals of WMO weather station 00098425, 3 km (2 miles) from Manila at 15 m (49 ft) above sea level, not the weather model’s.
