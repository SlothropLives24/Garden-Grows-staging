Frost dates for Cochabamba, Bolivia

Half of years have their last spring frost by September 15, but 1 year in 10 still frosts after October 13.

The Cochabamba weather station, less than 2 km (about a mile) from Cochabamba, recorded a frost in 18 of its 18 winters on record from 1991 to 2020.

From a weather model, not a station, checked against Cochabamba’s record · A good hunch

A station’s frost count with the model’s dates, graded A good hunch. Whether frost comes here, and how often, is the Cochabamba weather station’s own count, less than 2 km (about a mile) away. Its dates come from the weather model’s square centered 20 km (12 miles) away.

If you know your own frost dates, enter them under your place on the Plan, and your plans go by yours, with these shown beside them. See where every city’s dates come from on the coverage map.

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at -17.25, -66.25, 20 km (12 miles) from the center of Cochabamba. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

The frost count is a weather station’s. It comes from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network daily records (GHCN-Daily) for the Cochabamba station (BL000085223), less than 2 km (about a mile) from Cochabamba. A winter counts when the station recorded its three coldest months, and it is a frost winter when a night in it fell to 0 °C (32 °F) or below. Any frost dates on this page are the weather model’s.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.

The weather model reads this square on ground about 1,726 m (5,663 ft) higher than Cochabamba, where its temperatures run several degrees off, so this page shows the months it marks and leaves its numbers out.
