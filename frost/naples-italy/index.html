Frost dates for Naples, Italy

Half of years have their last spring frost by February 21, but 1 year in 10 still frosts after March 31.

A frost date is really a percentile, here from 30 years (1991 to 2020) of a weather model’s daily lows near Naples

From a weather model, not a station; no long record nearby to check it against · A good hunch

The weather model alone, graded A good hunch. Its dates come from the weather model’s square centered 44 km (27 miles) away.

If you know your own frost dates, enter them under your place on the Plan, and your plans go by yours, with these shown beside them. See where every city’s dates come from on the coverage map.

Modeled, not measured. No weather station stands behind these dates. They come from ERA5, a reconstruction of the world’s weather from 1991 to 2020 made by ECMWF, Europe’s medium-range weather forecasting agency, for the EU’s Copernicus program, and read here through Open-Meteo. They are the model’s daily lows for the point at 40.75, 14.75, 44 km (27 miles) from the center of Naples. A frost here means a low at or below 0 °C (32 °F).

The model’s point for Naples itself lies over the sea, where the water keeps the nights milder than on land, so these dates come from the nearest point over land, the one beside it.

A weather model sees the land in squares about 25 km (16 miles) across, so it can miss what sets one garden apart from the next: a frost hollow, a hillside, a lakeshore, a city’s own warmth. The Milpa planner reads these same numbers for a garden here and grades them A good hunch.

Cold air also pools in low ground on the clear, still nights when frost happens, so a hollow can freeze while a slope a hundred meters away does not. No table can know that about your garden. A few seasons of your own logged frosts can.

The weather model reads this square on ground about 450 m (1,476 ft) higher than Naples, where its temperatures run several degrees off, so this page shows the months it marks and leaves its numbers out.
