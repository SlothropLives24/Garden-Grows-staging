Perimeter trap crop

Cucumbers ringed by a border of winter squash that draws cucumber beetles away from the crop, so you can catch them there.

Space it needs

At least 8 m²86 ft². If your bed is smaller, the planner grays this plant team out, says why, and offers the nearest adaptation that fits. It never swaps in something else without telling you.

What it looks like planted

Between seasons

Nothing is left living in the bed over winter; the whole planting is re-established.

What it claims

A ring of the cucurbit the beetles prefer draws them to the edge, where they are dealt with, and keeps most of them and the wilt they carry off the crop

Promising

How it works: Striped cucumber beetles prefer Cucurbita maxima to C. pepo and C. moschata, and they arrive in one early flush from whatever direction they overwintered. A perimeter of the preferred host intercepts that flush on every side and concentrates the beetles on plants the gardener can treat or strip, so the unsprayed center sees far fewer beetles and far less of the wilt bacterium they transmit. The trap concentrates the beetles and does not kill them. The beetles in the border must be removed, or the ring feeds the crop instead of protecting it.

Evidence: checked against 3 sources in the research-based literature.

What to keep in mind

The border does the catching; you do the rest. Beetles gathered on the hubbard have to be removed, by hand, under a cover, or with whatever control you choose. Otherwise the ring becomes a nursery that sends more beetles onto the crop. Plant the hubbard a week or two before the cucumbers so it is up when the first beetles arrive, and keep it unbroken around the crop. The trials behind this were field-scale; a garden ring is the same idea at a size the researchers only say may work.

Rules that decide where it fits

Perennial members must not be sited in ground that is annually turned or rotated.

Well established

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner won’t allow it.

How it works: Tillage destroys the crown. Rotation moves the annuals; the perennial cannot move.
