What it claims

Understory insectary improves predator presence

A good hunch

How it works: The flowers supply nectar and pollen that adult predators and parasitoids feed on, so a patch of them near a fruiting crop keeps those beneficial insects fed and close by to work the crop. The effect is real but not guaranteed. An insectary patch does not always suppress pests.

Evidence: checked against 1 source in the research-based literature.

The source

ipm.ucanr.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Insectary Plants UC Statewide IPM Program (UC IPM)
Insectary plants are those grown to attract, feed, and shelter insect parasites (parasitoids) and predators to enhance biological pest control. Insectary plants provide nectar and pollen, which the adults of many natural enemy species need to consume.

Don't plant anything sensitive to walnut toxin (juglone) within a walnut tree's root zone. Nightshades are sensitive, and so are apple and cabbage.

Well established

If you skip this, the planting fails. The planner won’t allow it.

How it works: Juglans roots exude hydrojuglone, oxidized to juglone, a respiration inhibitor. The nightshades are highly sensitive; they wilt and die.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

hort.extension.wisc.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Black Walnut Toxicity UW-Madison / Wisconsin Horticulture (Joy, Hudelson & Jull, rev. 2024-02-28)
Vegetables such as tomato, potato, eggplant and pepper ... are particularly sensitive to juglone

What plants are sensitive to the juglone produced by black walnuts? Iowa State Univ. Extension & Outreach, Yard and Garden FAQ

Remedy

Plant it outside the walnut's root zone, which takes in the whole drip line and beyond. There is no soil treatment for juglone.

Some plants can't fruit from their own pollen, so they need a second plant of a different variety nearby.

Well established

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Many fruit trees carry self-incompatibility alleles at a single locus. When a pollen grain shares an allele with the pistil it lands on, the pistil rejects it and no seed sets. A plant's own pollen always shares its alleles, so it cannot fertilize itself. Neither can a second tree of the identical variety, which is genetically the same. Fertilization needs pollen from a genetically distinct plant.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.psu.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Pollination Requirements for Various Fruits and Nuts Penn State Extension
Chestnut, Chinese: All are self-sterile. Two or more varieties or seedling trees are necessary to ensure adequate cross-pollination.

pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov Accessed July 11, 2026

Pollen-Pistil Interaction in Response to Pollination Variants in Subtropical Japanese Plum (Prunus salicina Lindl.) Varieties
Japanese plum exhibits gametophytic self-incompatibility (GSI), a prezygotic reproductive barrier where genetically related pollen is rejected by the pistil ... governed by a polymorphic locus (S) ... Self-incompatibility occurs when the same S-allele is expressed in the haploid pollen grain and the diploid pistil.
Remedy

Plant at least two, and where the species needs a second cultivar make them different varieties. Two of the same will not pollinate each other.

Don't follow a Verticillium host with raspberries, strawberries or stone fruit

Promising

If you skip this, you lose real yield. The planner warns you.

How it works: Verticillium dahliae persists in soil as microsclerotia and infects a wide host range spanning nightshades and rose family. There is no in-ground cure once a susceptible perennial is planted, so it can wilt and die from inoculum an earlier host left behind. Because the reservoir family may differ from the victim's, the same-family rotation intervals miss it.

Evidence: checked against 3 sources in the research-based literature.

The 3 sources

extension.psu.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Bramble Disease - Verticillium Wilt Penn State Extension
Verticillium wilt is caused by two common soilborne fungi (Verticillium dahliae and Verticillium albo-atrum) that have a wide host range

www.umass.edu Accessed July 12, 2026

Brambles - Diseases UMass Extension, New England Small Fruit Management Guide
Verticillium attacks a wide range of plants, including potatoes, tomatoes, peppers, eggplant and strawberries. It overwinters in the soil and on plant debris. Do not plant [raspberries following these crops].

Accessed July 11, 2026

Verticillium Wilt of Tomato and Eggplant NC State Extension
Verticillium Wilt of Tomato and Eggplant
Remedy

Site the perennial on ground with no recent nightshades, or choose one that is not Verticillium-susceptible. Extension advises growing a non-host (corn or a small grain) for at least two years first, without claiming that clears the soil. Once the perennial is planted there is no in-ground cure.
