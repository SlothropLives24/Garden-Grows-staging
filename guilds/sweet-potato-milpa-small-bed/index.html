Sweet-potato milpa (small bed)

Climbing beans on a trellis with sweet potato covering the ground, for a bed too small for corn.

Space it needs

At least 1.5 m²16 ft². If your bed is smaller, the planner grays this plant team out, says why, and offers the nearest adaptation that fits. It never swaps in something else without telling you.

Adapted from Sweet-potato milpa.

What it looks like planted

Between seasons

Nothing is left living in the bed over winter; the whole planting is re-established.

What it claims

Trellis provides vertical support

Well established

Sweet-potato foliage suppresses weeds and retains soil moisture

A good hunch

How it works: A living cover keeps a leaf canopy over the ground. It intercepts light at the soil surface, so weed seedlings are shaded out before they establish, and it slows surface evaporation, so the soil holds more water. In a Three Sisters planting this is the squash's role. Its broad leaves shade the bed.

Beans improve the ground for next season

Promising

How it works: A legume fixes atmospheric nitrogen into its tissue as it grows. When the crop is killed and its residue decomposes, soil microbes mineralize that organic nitrogen, converting a form plants can't use into one they can, and release it for the next crop. How much depends on how well the legume grew, and it is lower on sandy soils.

Evidence: checked against 2 sources in the research-based literature.

The 2 sources

extension.psu.edu Read by a person on July 13, 2026

Growing Cover Crops for Nitrogen on Vegetable Farms Penn State Extension
When cover crops are killed and begin to decompose, nitrogen can be released from the residues back into the soil where it can feed the next crop

cropsandsoils.extension.wisc.edu Accessed July 11, 2026

The nitrogen credit depends on the amount of crop growth and when the legume was planted. Nitrogen credits are 50 lb N/a lower on sandy, coarse-textured soils.

What to keep in mind

Label as an adaptation, not as a milpa. The beans climb a trellis instead of corn, so the corn block is gone, along with the pollination mechanism that needs it. Sweet potato remains the ground layer it always was.

Who does what, and the swaps

Each job in this planting can be done by more than one plant. The usual choice comes first, and each swap says what it costs.

Support

  • Or a structure: Cattle panel
  • What that swap costs: No biomass, no romance. About $20. Reusable across seasons.
  • Swaps: Sunflower
  • What a swap costs: A heavy nitrogen feeder that competes with the beans. It needs a ~30-day head start or the vines pull it down, and it holds up about 2 vines per stalk.

Fixer

  • Usual choice: Pole bean
  • Swaps: Runner bean, Cowpea, Pole lima

Cover

  • Usual choice: Sweet potato
